CHAPTER THREE

The Cost of Being Changed

Following Jesus means surrendering what we want when obedience requires us to trust Him more than ourselves.

Chapter Three • Surrender and Obedience
"Change becomes real when surrender becomes real."

It is easy to ask Jesus to change our lives. It is harder to let Him decide what needs to change. We may want the peace, freedom, and transformation He offers while still holding tightly to our own plans, preferences, and desires. Sometimes we ask Jesus to change us while quietly hoping He will leave certain parts of our lives untouched.

That is where surrender begins.

Following Jesus does not mean that we earn His love or work for His grace. His grace is freely given, and salvation cannot be earned. But receiving His grace also means becoming willing to follow Him, and following Him will sometimes require us to release things we would rather keep. It may be a habit, an attitude, a relationship, a plan, a desire, or simply our need to remain in control.

What Am I Unwilling to Release?

This is an uncomfortable question because we can become attached to things without realizing how tightly we are holding them. Sometimes we know something needs to change, yet we continue making excuses for it. We tell ourselves that we will deal with it later, that it is not really hurting anyone, or that we have a good reason for continuing. The longer we protect something, the easier it can become to convince ourselves that we are protecting ourselves rather than resisting change.

But surrender requires honesty. If Jesus is Lord, then I cannot give Him authority only over the parts of my life that I find convenient. I have to be willing to let Him speak into the areas I would rather protect, even when He reveals something I would rather not confront.

THE COST OF SURRENDER

I Cannot Follow Jesus and Follow Myself.

There will be moments when my desires and His direction do not agree. In those moments, surrender means choosing His will over my own and trusting that His wisdom is greater than my understanding.

This is where obedience becomes more than a word. I can say that Jesus is Lord, but eventually my choices have to demonstrate whether I believe it. I can say that I trust Him, but trust becomes visible when I obey even without knowing how everything will turn out.

Obedience is not always comfortable. Sometimes it requires an apology when I would rather defend myself, forgiveness when I would rather remain angry, or walking away from something familiar because I know it is pulling me away from Jesus. Sometimes obedience means waiting when I want an answer immediately. There are moments when following Jesus requires me to say yes when everything inside me wants to say no.

The Hardest Part Is Often Control.

I like knowing what comes next. I like having an explanation, understanding what people mean, and knowing how a situation will end before I take the first step. There is comfort in certainty because certainty makes us feel prepared, but faith does not always provide that kind of certainty.

Sometimes Jesus asks us to move before we understand the entire path. He may give us enough light for the next step without showing us everything that comes afterward. That can be difficult because surrender means accepting that I am not the one who sees everything. I only see what is in front of me. Jesus sees what I cannot.

There have been times when I wanted an explanation from God when what I really needed was trust. I wanted to know why something happened, why a door closed, why a relationship changed, or why a situation did not turn out the way I expected. Sometimes the answer did not come immediately. What did come was the opportunity to trust Jesus anyway.

That is where faith becomes real. I do not have to understand the entire journey before I take the next step with Him.

A PERSONAL QUESTION

Do I Trust Jesus Enough to Obey?

Trust is easy to claim when everything makes sense. The deeper test comes when obedience requires faith without an explanation.

Surrender does not mean that I stop asking questions. It means that my questions do not become conditions for obedience. I can ask Jesus why, admit that I do not understand, and tell Him that I am afraid while still choosing to follow Him. I may not understand what He is doing, but I can trust the One who sees what I cannot.

Obedience Has a Cost.

Jesus never promised that following Him would always be easy. He called His followers to deny themselves, take up their cross, and follow Him. That invitation is not about losing our identity. It is about surrendering our right to make ourselves the highest authority in our own lives.

There is a difference between being controlled by other people and willingly submitting ourselves to Jesus. One is bondage; the other is trust. When I surrender to Jesus, I am not saying that I have no value. I am saying that I trust the One who created me more than I trust my own limited understanding.

That changes the way I see obedience. Obedience is not punishment; it is a response to love. If Jesus has truly become Lord of my life, then His instructions cannot remain suggestions that I follow only when they fit my plans.

There have been times when I wanted the benefits of following Jesus without accepting the cost. I wanted His peace without surrendering my control, His direction without giving up my plans, and His forgiveness without confronting the things I needed to change. I wanted Jesus to bless the life I had already chosen rather than asking Him to lead the life I was willing to surrender.

But Jesus does not call me to a convenient faith. He calls me to Himself.

Surrender Is a Daily Decision.

I have learned that surrender is not something I can decide once and never revisit. New circumstances reveal new areas where I need to trust Jesus. One day I may need to surrender fear, another day I may need to surrender anger, and another day I may need to surrender my plans. Sometimes I may discover that something I thought I had already given to Jesus is still sitting in my hands.

When that happens, I have another choice. I can hold on, or I can open my hands again. That is not failure. It is another opportunity to trust Jesus with something I have been trying to carry myself.

SURRENDER

"Jesus, You Can Have This Too."

A surrendered heart keeps opening its hands. Whatever Jesus reveals, we bring it to Him instead of protecting it from Him.

There are things we may not want to surrender because we believe they are protecting us. We hold onto resentment because it makes us feel justified. We hold onto control because uncertainty scares us. We hold onto approval because rejection hurts. We hold onto our plans because changing them feels like losing something we worked hard to build.

But whatever controls us cannot be surrendered halfway. Jesus wants us free, and that freedom begins when we stop treating our fears, wounds, preferences, and desires as greater authorities than His Word.

What Does Obedience Look Like Today?

Obedience is often much more practical than we make it. It may mean telling the truth when a lie would be easier, apologizing without waiting for someone else to apologize first, forgiving instead of rehearsing an offense, refusing to return hurt with hurt, choosing patience when frustration rises, or walking away from something that continually pulls us away from Jesus.

It may also mean doing what is right even when nobody will know that we did it. These decisions may not look dramatic from the outside, but they matter because obedience is built through choices. A changed life is not created by one impressive moment; it is formed through countless ordinary decisions to follow Jesus.

I Am Still Learning.

I do not write this as someone who has mastered surrender. I still have moments when I want my own way, when obedience is uncomfortable, and when I want an explanation before I trust. But I am learning not to confuse struggle with permission to stop.

When I recognize that I am resisting Jesus, I can stop and surrender again. When I fail, I can repent. When I do not understand, I can trust. When I am afraid, I can pray. When I am tempted to return to an old pattern, I can choose a different response. None of this means that I have mastered the journey; it means I am willing to keep walking with Jesus through it.

THE CALL TO SURRENDER

"If anyone wishes to follow Me, he must deny himself, take up his cross daily, and follow Me."

Luke 9:23

Jesus did not say that following Him would cost nothing. He asked for our lives. That does not mean He wants to destroy who we are; it means He wants our lives placed fully into His hands. He can work with what we surrender, transform what we place before Him, and use what we once thought was too broken, too complicated, or too ordinary.

The challenge is that we have to open our hands. We have to trust Him enough to believe that what He asks us to release is not greater than what He is capable of doing with the life we place in His hands.

I Choose Jesus.

There will always be reasons to hold back. There will always be things I would rather control and moments when obedience feels harder than doing what I want. But I do not want to spend my life protecting the person I used to be. I want to keep becoming more like Jesus, even when that means surrendering something that has become comfortable or familiar.

That means surrendering again when I need to, choosing obedience when comfort would be easier, trusting when I cannot see the outcome, and allowing Jesus to have the final word. It means recognizing that following Him is not about controlling the journey but about trusting the One who leads it.

The cost of being changed is surrender, but surrender places my life into hands I can trust. What Jesus asks me to release can never compare with what He is teaching me to receive. When I surrender my plans, my fears, my control, and the things I have been holding too tightly, I am not losing everything. I am learning to trust Jesus with everything.

I cannot control the entire journey, but I can choose who I will follow. Today, I choose Jesus.
