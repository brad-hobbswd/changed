CHAPTER THREE

The Cost of Being Changed

Following Jesus may cost us the approval of the world, but it always leads us closer to the heart of God.

"When Jesus changes your life, you will not always lose the crowd. You will always gain the courage to follow Him wherever He leads."

One of the greatest misunderstandings about following Jesus is the belief that surrendering our lives to Him will remove every hardship and replace every struggle with comfort. While Christ certainly gives us peace, hope, and joy that the world cannot offer, He never promised that discipleship would be free from sacrifice. In fact, the closer we walk with Him, the more we discover that a transformed life often comes with a cost.

That cost is not God's way of punishing us. It is the natural result of belonging to a kingdom that is not of this world.

When Jesus changes your heart, your values begin to change. The things that once satisfied you begin to lose their appeal because your desires are no longer rooted in the temporary pleasures of this life. You begin to hunger for righteousness, to love truth, and to pursue holiness. Those changes may seem quiet at first, but they eventually become visible to the people around you.

Not everyone will understand those changes.

Some will celebrate what God is doing in your life, but others may question it. They may wonder why your priorities have shifted or why you no longer participate in conversations, habits, or relationships that once seemed normal. There may even be people who accuse you of thinking you are better than they are, when in reality you are simply trying to follow the One who rescued you.

I have learned that one of the hardest parts of following Christ is not resisting the world. It is accepting that the world may no longer understand you.

There have been times in my own life when I wanted nothing more than to be understood. I wanted people to know my heart, to recognize my intentions, and to see that my desire was never to hurt anyone but to live honestly before God. Yet I eventually realized that if Jesus, who was perfect in every way, was misunderstood by those around Him, I should not be surprised when I experience the same reality.

That realization did not make rejection enjoyable, but it gave it purpose.

That question changed everything.

Instead of asking, "Why don't people understand me?" I began asking, "Lord, how can I remain faithful to You even when they do not?"

Faithfulness is not measured by how many people agree with us. It is measured by our willingness to obey God when obedience is costly.

FAITHFULNESS

Faithfulness Over Popularity

Throughout history, the men and women whom God used most powerfully were rarely those who sought the approval of the crowd. Noah spent years building an ark while surrounded by people who mocked his obedience. Moses chose to identify with God's people instead of enjoying the temporary pleasures of Pharaoh's palace. Daniel remained faithful in Babylon even when faithfulness placed him in a den of lions. The apostles continued preaching Christ despite imprisonment, persecution, and the constant threat of death.

They stood because they believed that obedience to God was worth more than acceptance by the world.

That same choice confronts every believer.

There will be moments when compromise appears easier than conviction. There will be opportunities to remain silent when truth needs to be spoken with love. There will be seasons when standing for Christ may cost friendships, opportunities, or even your reputation. Those moments reveal what truly governs our hearts.

Jesus never asked His followers to seek suffering, but He did call them to carry their cross. A cross is not a symbol of convenience. It is a symbol of surrender. Every day we choose whether we will follow our own desires or submit ourselves to the will of God. That choice is rarely made once. It is made again and again throughout the Christian life.

For me, there were seasons when I questioned whether it was worth continuing to stand for what I believed was right. Misunderstandings, loneliness, and disappointment can wear down even the strongest believer. Yet every time I opened the Scriptures, I found the same invitation.

Jesus was not calling me to popularity.

He was calling me to faithfulness.

There is a freedom that comes when you stop measuring your life by the opinions of others and begin measuring it by your obedience to Christ. That freedom does not make criticism disappear, but it changes its influence over your heart. You no longer live for applause because your greatest desire is to hear the words, "Well done, thou good and faithful servant."

The world often tells us to follow our hearts, but Scripture teaches us to follow Christ. Our hearts can be influenced by fear, pride, or emotion. Christ never changes. He remains faithful when our feelings fluctuate, and He remains true when the opinions of people shift with every passing season.

Being changed does not mean that we become fearless.

It means that our faith becomes greater than our fear.

Courage is not the absence of uncertainty. Courage is choosing to obey God even when the outcome is unknown.

Perhaps you are reading this chapter while carrying the weight of misunderstanding. Maybe you have lost relationships because you chose to honor Christ. Perhaps you feel as though your commitment to biblical truth has left you standing in places where very few people are willing to stand beside you.

If that is where you find yourself today,

Remember that you are never standing alone.

The same Lord who called Noah, Abraham, Moses, Daniel, Peter, Paul, and countless faithful believers throughout history walks with you today. He has not forgotten you, and He has not abandoned you. The road of discipleship may sometimes feel lonely, but it is never empty because Christ Himself has promised to be with His people always.

THE PROMISE OF HIS PRESENCE

The Cost Is Real. So Is His Presence.

Following Jesus comes with a real cost. The sacrifices, the misunderstandings, and the challenges are real. But so is His presence. His grace is sufficient. His promises are certain. His reward is eternal.

When everything is weighed in the balance, there is no sacrifice too great for the One who gave everything to save us.

A life changed by Christ will sometimes stand apart from the crowd,

But it will never stand apart from the presence of God.

That is the confidence that allows us to remain faithful, not only when obedience is easy, but also when it costs us everything.

And in the end, there is no greater privilege than to know that the One who changed our lives is also the One who faithfully walks beside us every step of the journey.

THE COST OF DISCIPLESHIP
