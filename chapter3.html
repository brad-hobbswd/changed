CHAPTER THREE

The Cost of Being Changed

Following Jesus means more than asking Him to change us. It means becoming willing to surrender what He asks us to surrender.

Chapter Three • Surrender and Obedience
"Change becomes real when surrender becomes real."

There is something we have to understand if we are serious about being changed by Jesus Christ: transformation has a cost. Grace is freely given, salvation cannot be earned, and the love of Jesus is not something we can purchase, but following Him will still require us to surrender things we would rather keep. There will be moments when Jesus asks us to let go of attitudes, habits, relationships, expectations, pride, bitterness, fear, or even plans that we have spent years building. The difficult part is not always knowing what needs to change. Sometimes the difficult part is being willing to let it go.

I know what it is like to hold tightly to what is familiar. When you have spent much of your life feeling different or misunderstood, familiarity can become a place of safety. You learn what works for you, what makes sense to you, what helps you feel comfortable, and what allows you to navigate a world that does not always understand the way you experience it. As an autistic person, I have learned that predictability and familiarity can matter deeply. When things change unexpectedly, when communication becomes confusing, or when people respond in ways I did not anticipate, it can be difficult to process what is happening.

There is nothing wrong with recognizing those realities. Understanding myself has helped me become more aware of how I experience the world. It has helped me recognize areas where I need patience, where I need clearer communication, and where I may need to step away from an overwhelming situation before I can respond well. But there is another lesson I have had to learn, and that lesson reaches beyond autism.

I cannot allow what is familiar to become more important than what Jesus is asking of me.

That is where surrender becomes personal.

Jesus does not only ask for what is easy to give.

It would be wonderful if following Jesus always meant giving Him the things we were already ready to release. It would be easy if obedience only required us to surrender the things we had already decided were bad for us. But sometimes Jesus puts His finger on something we have become comfortable carrying. Sometimes He reveals something that we have defended for so long that we no longer recognize it as something that needs to change.

It may be pride.

It may be resentment.

It may be the need to be understood.

It may be the desire to prove that we are right.

It may be our need for control.

It may be our fear of what will happen if we trust Him completely.

For me, one of the hardest things to surrender has been the desire to be understood. When you have spent years feeling misunderstood, there can be a strong temptation to explain yourself over and over again. You want people to know your intentions. You want them to understand why you responded the way you did. You want them to know the parts of your story they cannot see.

Sometimes that desire is completely understandable. Sometimes we really have been misunderstood. Sometimes people have judged us without knowing the whole story. Sometimes they have made assumptions about us that were never true.

But I have learned that I cannot spend my entire life trying to convince everyone to understand me.

Jesus already does.

That does not mean I stop communicating. It does not mean I stop standing for truth. It does not mean I accept false accusations or pretend that harmful behavior is acceptable. It means I no longer need the approval of every person in order to know who I am.

That is freedom.

THE COST OF SURRENDER

Letting Go of Control

Surrender means trusting Jesus even when I cannot see the entire path ahead. It means allowing Him to lead instead of demanding that He explain everything before I obey.

Control can feel like safety. If I know what is happening, what comes next, what people expect, and how everything is supposed to work, I feel prepared. But life does not always cooperate with our plans, and following Jesus certainly does not mean that He will give us every answer before asking us to take the next step.

Faith requires movement before complete understanding.

That can be difficult for anyone, but it has been especially meaningful for me because I often want things to make sense. I want to understand why something happened. I want to know what comes next. I want to understand what another person meant. I want to know why God allowed something to happen the way it did.

Sometimes I have prayed for an explanation when Jesus was offering me an invitation to trust Him instead.

That is a hard lesson.

Jesus does not always explain the entire journey. Sometimes He simply says, "Follow Me."

Following Him means that I may not know what tomorrow looks like, but I can know who holds tomorrow. I may not understand every circumstance, but I can trust the One who sees what I cannot see. I may not understand why a door closed, but I can believe that Jesus is still leading me.

That kind of trust requires surrender.

And surrender requires obedience.

Obedience is one of the clearest places where our faith becomes visible. It is easy to say that we love Jesus. It is easy to say that we trust Him. It is easy to sing about surrender in church. But eventually our lives have to answer the question our mouths are asking.

If I say I trust Jesus, will I obey Him when obedience costs me something?

If I say I love Jesus, will I follow Him when following Him means giving up what I want?

If I say that Jesus is Lord, am I actually allowing Him to lead my life?

Those questions are uncomfortable because they leave very little room for pretending.

Jesus is not looking for a performance. He is looking for surrender.

That means I cannot simply give Him the parts of my life that are convenient while keeping the rest locked away. I cannot say, "Jesus, You can have this part of me, but this part belongs to me." I cannot ask Him to change my life while refusing to let Him change my choices.

There comes a moment when surrender becomes all or nothing.

Jesus wants all of me.

That does not mean I will suddenly become perfect. It means I stop making excuses for the areas where I know He is calling me to grow. It means that when He reveals something in me that does not belong, I do not immediately defend it. I bring it to Him.

"Lord, this is in me. Change it."

"Lord, I do not want to forgive. Teach me how."

"Lord, I am angry. Help me respond without allowing anger to control me."

"Lord, I am afraid. Teach me to trust You."

"Lord, I do not understand. Help me obey You even when I do not understand."

Those prayers are not signs of weakness. They are signs that surrender is becoming real.

A QUESTION OF SURRENDER

What Am I Still Holding?

What have I asked Jesus to change while secretly refusing to release it?

What familiar thing am I afraid to surrender because I do not know what will replace it?

What part of my life do I still want to control instead of trusting Jesus to lead?

Those questions can reveal uncomfortable things, but uncomfortable truth is still truth. Jesus cannot transform what I refuse to acknowledge. If I hide my heart from Him, I am not protecting myself. I am protecting the very things He wants to heal.

That is why honesty before Jesus matters.

I do not have to come to Him pretending that I have everything together. I do not have to clean myself up before I approach Him. I do not have to become perfect before I can begin the journey of transformation.

I simply have to come honestly.

Jesus already knows what is there.

He knows the thoughts I do not say aloud. He knows the wounds I try to hide. He knows the fears I do not want anyone else to see. He knows the things I have surrendered and the things I am still holding onto.

And He still calls me.

That is grace.

Grace does not tell me that I can remain unchanged. Grace tells me that I do not have to remain unchanged.

There is a difference.

Jesus does not condemn me because I am still growing. He invites me to continue growing. He does not abandon me because I still struggle. He teaches me how to bring those struggles to Him. He does not turn away because I have failed. He calls me to repentance and gives me the opportunity to begin again.

That is why I cannot confuse perfection with transformation.

Transformation is a process.

There are days when I get it right, and there are days when I do not. There are moments when I respond with patience and moments when I realize afterward that I responded out of frustration. There are times when I trust Jesus completely and other times when fear reminds me that I am still learning.

But the difference is that I do not want to stay where I was.

I want Jesus more than I want my old ways.

That desire matters.

THE DIFFERENCE

Different Is Not the Goal.

The goal is not simply to be different from the world. The goal is to belong completely to Jesus and allow His character to become visible through my life.

This is where the message that first inspired Changed became personal for me. The idea of being different made me think about my own life and all the ways I have always experienced the world differently. But Jesus began taking me somewhere deeper. He showed me that being different does not automatically mean being transformed.

I could be different and still be angry.

I could be different and still be prideful.

I could be different and still refuse to forgive.

I could be different and still live for the approval of people.

I could be different and still resist obedience.

Jesus was showing me that the question was never simply whether I was different.

The question was whether I was changed.

And if I truly want to be changed, I have to be willing to surrender.

There is no way around it.

Following Jesus will cost me my right to live as though I am my own master. It will cost me the freedom to justify every reaction simply because I feel strongly about it. It will cost me the comfort of always choosing what is familiar. It will sometimes cost me relationships, approval, opportunities, or plans that I thought were part of my future.

But Jesus never promised that surrender would leave us empty.

He promises something greater.

He gives us Himself.

What I surrender to Jesus is never more valuable than what I receive from Him.

When I surrender pride, I gain humility.

When I surrender bitterness, I make room for forgiveness.

When I surrender fear, I learn trust.

When I surrender control, I discover faith.

When I surrender the need to be accepted by everyone, I discover that being known by Jesus is enough.

And when I surrender my plans, I begin to discover that His plans are not limited by what I can see.

Surrender is not losing.

Surrender is trusting.

There is another part of surrender that I am still learning. Sometimes I have to surrender my understanding of myself. Not my identity, but my assumptions about what my life has to look like. I have spent years trying to understand why I am the way I am, why certain things are harder for me, why some people understand me while others do not, and why certain experiences have shaped me so deeply.

Those questions matter, but they cannot become the center of my identity.

My identity is not autism.

My identity is not rejection.

My identity is not misunderstanding.

My identity is not failure.

My identity is not the opinions people have formed about me.

My identity is found in Jesus Christ.

That does not erase the reality of my experiences. It puts them in their proper place.

I can acknowledge that I am autistic without believing that autism defines the limits of what Jesus can do through my life. I can acknowledge that I have been misunderstood without becoming controlled by the need to prove myself. I can acknowledge that I have weaknesses without believing that my weaknesses make me useless to God.

Jesus can work through the person I actually am.

He does not need a manufactured version of me.

He wants my surrendered heart.

SURRENDER

"Therefore, brothers and sisters, I urge you, in view of the mercies of God, to offer your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God, which is your reasonable service."

Romans 12:1

A living sacrifice cannot remain untouched.

When I place my life before Jesus, I am giving Him permission to work in places I would rather protect. I am telling Him that His will matters more than mine. I am saying that I trust Him enough to follow even when I cannot see the entire road.

That is what changed begins to look like.

Not perfection.

Not pretending.

Not becoming someone else.

Surrender.

And surrender is not a single decision that I make once and never revisit. There are new things to surrender as I grow. There are deeper places where Jesus asks for trust. There are moments when I discover that something I thought I had given to Him is still sitting in my hands.

So I give it back.

Again.

And again.

Because I would rather spend my life becoming more like Jesus than spend my life protecting the person I used to be.

I do not want to remain the same.

I do not want to look back years from now and realize that I spent my entire Christian life asking Jesus to bless the person I refused to surrender. I do not want to know Scripture without allowing Scripture to change me. I do not want to talk about Jesus without actually following Him. I do not want to say that I love Him while continually refusing to obey Him.

I want my life to tell the truth about what my mouth says I believe.

That means there will be a cost.

There will be things I have to release.

There will be attitudes I have to confront.

There will be wounds I have to bring to Jesus.

There will be moments when obedience is harder than comfort.

There will be moments when following Jesus means walking away from what feels familiar and trusting Him to lead me somewhere I have never been.

And I want to go.

Not because I am strong enough to do it alone, but because I know I am not alone.

Jesus is with me.

He has been with me in every season. He has been with me when I felt different. He has been with me when I felt misunderstood. He has been with me when I failed. He has been with me when I questioned myself. He has been with me when I did not understand what He was doing.

And He is still changing me.

That is why I can surrender.

Because the One asking me to surrender

is the One I can trust.

The cost of being changed is real, but so is the grace that carries us through it. Jesus does not ask us to surrender because He wants to take everything good away from us. He asks us to surrender because He knows what is destroying us, what is distracting us, and what is keeping us from becoming who He has called us to be.

Sometimes the thing I am afraid to give up is the very thing preventing me from receiving what Jesus wants to give me.

So I have a choice.

I can keep holding on to what is familiar, or I can open my hands and trust Him.

I can keep defending the person I have always been, or I can allow Jesus to continue forming me.

I can keep asking Him to change everyone else, or I can invite Him to begin with me.

I can remain different without ever being transformed, or I can surrender my heart and allow Jesus to make His presence visible through my life.

I choose Jesus.

And that choice is not made once.

It is made every day.

Every morning gives me another opportunity to surrender.

Every difficult conversation gives me another opportunity to choose grace.

Every disappointment gives me another opportunity to trust.

Every failure gives me another opportunity to repent.

Every victory gives me another opportunity to give Jesus the glory.

This is what it means to be changed.

The cost is surrender, but the reward is a life that no longer belongs to itself. When Jesus has our hearts, He can begin changing everything that flows from them.
