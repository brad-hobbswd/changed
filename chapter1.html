CHAPTER ONE

Changed

Being different is not the same as being changed. Jesus does not call us to become someone else. He calls us to surrender our hearts and allow Him to transform our lives from the inside out.

Chapter One • The Beginning of Transformation
"Does Jesus really want to change me, or does He want me to become more like Him?"

For much of my life, I have known what it feels like to be different. I have often experienced the world in ways that other people did not understand, and there have been moments when I wondered why certain things seemed natural to everyone else while they felt much more complicated to me. As an autistic person, I have learned that the way I process information, communicate, respond to situations, and understand the world around me can sometimes be different from what people expect. That difference has shaped my story, but it does not define my worth, and it does not determine how much Jesus can do in my life.

There have been times when being different felt less like a gift and more like a burden. Being misunderstood can make a person question himself. Being judged by expectations that do not account for how he experiences the world can make him wonder whether he needs to change simply to be accepted. I have felt that tension in my own life, and I know what it is like to look around and wonder why fitting in seems easier for everyone else.

For a long time, I thought the answer might be to become more like the people around me. Maybe if I communicated differently, responded differently, thought differently, or learned how to hide the things that made me stand out, I would finally feel like I belonged. Yet the more I have walked with Jesus, the more I have realized that He was never asking me to become somebody else.

Jesus did not call me

to become someone else.

He called me to become more like Him.

That distinction matters because being different and being changed are not the same thing. My autism is part of my story, but it is not my identity. I do not need Jesus to erase the way I was created so that I can become acceptable to other people. I need Jesus to transform the places in my heart that do not reflect Him. I need Him to teach me how to love when loving is difficult, how to forgive when forgiveness feels impossible, how to obey when obedience costs me something, and how to trust Him when I do not understand what He is doing.

Jesus knows me completely. He knows how I think, how I process things, what overwhelms me, what brings me joy, where I struggle, and where I still need to grow. Nothing about me is hidden from Him. That means I do not have to approach Him pretending to be someone I am not. I can come to Him honestly and allow Him to work within the person I actually am.

That does not mean I can use being different as an excuse to avoid growth or obedience. Following Jesus means allowing Him to challenge every part of my life, including the parts I would rather defend. There are attitudes that need to change. There are reactions that need to be surrendered. There are desires that need to come under His authority. There are moments when I have to admit that I am wrong, ask for forgiveness, and choose obedience even when my feelings tell me to do something else.

That is what makes the word changed so important to me.

I first began thinking more deeply about this word after hearing Different by Christian recording artist Micah Tyler. The song challenged me to think about what it really means to live differently because of Jesus. It made me think about the kind of life I want to live and the kind of person I want others to see when they look at me.

But as I continued thinking, praying, and searching Scripture, I realized that simply being different was not the deepest question. I could be different because of my personality. I could be different because I am autistic. I could be different because of my experiences, my interests, my opinions, or the way I see the world. None of those things automatically mean that Jesus has changed my heart.

The question became much more personal.

Has Jesus truly changed me?

That question stayed with me because it forced me to look beyond appearances. It made me consider whether my faith was simply something I talked about or whether it was actually changing the way I lived. I could say that I love Jesus, but does my life demonstrate that love? I could say that I trust Him, but do I trust Him when He asks me to surrender something I desperately want to control? I could say that I believe His Word, but am I willing to obey it when obedience is uncomfortable?

Those questions are not easy, but they are necessary.

DIFFERENT IS NOT THE GOAL

Changed Is.

The purpose of following Jesus is not to become different simply for the sake of standing apart from everyone else. It is to become more like Him. Our personalities may be different. Our experiences may be different. Our abilities may be different. The way we process the world may be different. Yet every believer is called to allow Jesus Christ to transform the heart.

The world often teaches us to change ourselves so that we can become more successful, more accepted, more attractive, or more comfortable. We are encouraged to create the version of ourselves that receives the most approval. Jesus offers something completely different. He does not ask us to build an image that impresses people. He asks us to surrender the person we are to Him and allow Him to make us new.

That kind of change reaches deeper than behavior.

Jesus can change the way I respond when I am frustrated. He can change the way I treat someone who has hurt me. He can change the way I handle correction. He can change the way I respond when I feel rejected. He can change what I pursue, what I value, what I fear, and what I am willing to surrender. He can take the places in my life where I have been controlled by pride, anger, fear, selfishness, or the need for approval and teach me to place those things into His hands.

This is why being changed cannot simply mean looking different on the outside. A person can change his appearance without changing his heart. A person can change his vocabulary without changing his character. A person can attend church every week without surrendering his life to Jesus. A person can know Scripture and still resist the One who gave it.

Real change begins deeper than that.

It begins when I stop trying to convince myself that I am already everything I need to be and admit that I need Jesus. It begins when I allow Him to show me the truth about myself, even when that truth is uncomfortable. It begins when I stop asking Him only to change my circumstances and start asking Him to change me.

Sometimes I want Jesus

to change everything around me.

But sometimes He wants to begin with me.

That realization has changed the way I understand difficult seasons. Not every painful circumstance is something I caused, and not every struggle means that God is punishing me. Life can be difficult even when we are following Jesus faithfully. People can misunderstand us. Doors can close. Relationships can hurt. Plans can fall apart. We can experience rejection even when our intentions are good.

Yet Jesus can still use those moments to change us.

He can teach us patience when we want immediate answers. He can teach us humility when we want to prove that we are right. He can teach us forgiveness when we want to hold onto anger. He can teach us dependence when we want to control everything ourselves. He can teach us that His presence is more valuable than the approval of people.

I have learned that being misunderstood does not mean that Jesus has misunderstood me. People may not always understand why I respond the way I do, why certain things affect me differently, or why I experience situations in ways they would not expect. Jesus does not need an explanation. He already knows me completely.

That truth has given me freedom.

I do not have to spend my life trying to prove that I am exactly like everyone else. I do not have to be ashamed of being autistic. I do not have to pretend that certain things are easy for me when they are not. I can be honest about who I am while still allowing Jesus to work on who I am becoming.

That is an important difference.

Acceptance of who I am does not mean refusing to grow. Growth does not mean rejecting who God created me to be. Following Jesus means learning to hold both truths together. I can acknowledge the person God created me to be while surrendering every part of my life that does not reflect the character of Jesus.

The goal is not perfection.

The goal is surrender.

I am still learning what that means. I still fail. I still make mistakes. I still have moments when my words are stronger than my actions. I still have moments when I know what Jesus is asking me to do and struggle to do it. There are times when I become frustrated with myself because I know better and still choose poorly.

But failure does not mean that Jesus has stopped working.

It means I have another reason to return to Him.

His grace does not give me permission to remain unchanged. His grace gives me the opportunity to get back up, repent, learn, and continue following Him. When I fall, I do not have to hide from Jesus. I can come back to Him, confess what I have done, receive His forgiveness, and allow Him to continue the work He started in me.

WHEN PEOPLE SEE ME

Let Them See Jesus.

The older I get and the more I walk with Jesus, the more I realize that I do not want my life to be remembered simply because I was different. I want it to point beyond me. I want the way I love, forgive, serve, speak, obey, and trust God to make people curious about the One who is changing me.

I do not want people to see a perfect person because I am not one. I want them to see someone who has been surrendered to Jesus and who is still allowing Him to work. I want my life to point toward His grace rather than my accomplishments.

That is where the message that inspired this book becomes personal for me. The question is no longer simply whether I am different. The deeper question is whether the difference people see in my life points to Jesus.

If I am patient, let it point to Jesus. If I forgive, let it point to Jesus. If I serve someone who cannot repay me, let it point to Jesus. If I stand for truth when it would be easier to remain silent, let it point to Jesus. If I fail and then humble myself enough to repent, let even that point to Jesus.

Because the purpose of a changed life is not to make ourselves look impressive.

It is to make Jesus visible.

That is the kind of change I want.

I want Jesus to change the places in me that need to change without making me ashamed of the person He created me to be. I want Him to teach me how to live faithfully as an autistic man while becoming more like Him every day. I want my differences to be understood rather than hidden, while my character continues to be transformed by His grace.

I want to become less controlled by myself and more surrendered to Him. I want to stop measuring my life by the approval of people and start measuring it by obedience to Jesus. I want my faith to become more than something I say. I want it to become something people can see in the way I live.

That is what I mean by changed.

It does not mean that I have become perfect. It does not mean that every struggle has disappeared. It does not mean that I no longer have weaknesses, questions, or difficult days. It means that I am no longer willing to remain exactly as I was when Jesus found me.

I want Him to keep working.

I want Him to keep correcting me.

I want Him to keep teaching me.

I want Him to keep changing me.

And I want that change to become visible, not so that people will praise me, but so that they will see the One who is doing the work.

That is why this book begins here.

Before we talk about the cost of change, the battles that come with it, the evidence of transformation, or what it means to continue living differently, we have to understand where change begins.

It begins in the heart.

It begins with surrender.

It begins with Jesus.

THE FOUNDATION

"Therefore if any person is in Christ, he is a new creation; the old things have passed away. Behold, the fresh and new has come."

2 Corinthians 5:17

The journey ahead is not about becoming someone else.

It is about becoming who Jesus is calling me to become.

And perhaps that is the question every one of us needs to carry into the next chapter.

Has Jesus truly changed me?

Not just my words, but my heart. Not simply what people see on the outside, but who I am becoming when I am alone with Jesus. Not whether I am different from everyone else, but whether my life is becoming more like Him.

The journey begins with that question, and the answer will require more than words. It will require surrender.
