CHAPTER ONE

Changed

Real transformation begins when we stop trying to change ourselves for the approval of others and allow Jesus Christ to change us from within.

Chapter One • The Beginning of Transformation
"Therefore if any person is in Christ, he is a new creation."

The word changed sounds simple, but genuine change reaches much deeper than the things people can see. We can change our circumstances, our appearance, our routines, and even the way other people see us without ever allowing anything within our hearts to change. Jesus offers something different. He does not simply call us to improve ourselves. He calls us to surrender ourselves to Him and allow Him to make us new.

That kind of transformation begins with honesty. Before we can recognize what Jesus wants to change, we have to be willing to acknowledge where we are. We have to become honest about the attitudes, desires, habits, and decisions that may not reflect Him. That can be uncomfortable because it is often easier to see what needs to change in someone else than to allow God to examine our own hearts.

I have learned this personally. There have been times when I wanted Jesus to change what was happening around me while avoiding what He was trying to change within me. I wanted difficult circumstances to end, relationships to become easier, and problems to disappear. Yet there were moments when the first thing Jesus wanted to change was not my situation, but my response to it.

Change Begins

With the Heart.

Jesus cares about what people cannot see. He cares about the thoughts behind our words, the motives behind our actions, and the condition of our hearts when nobody else is watching. Outward behavior can be changed for a season, but lasting transformation reaches deeper than appearances.

This does not mean that following Jesus makes us perfect. It means that we become willing to let Him continue working in us. When He reveals something that needs to change, we do not have to hide it or defend it. We can bring it to Him, repent, receive His grace, and allow Him to teach us a better way to live.

THE HEART OF CHANGE

Jesus Changes What We Surrender.

Transformation begins when we stop asking Jesus to bless everything we want and become willing to surrender everything to Him.

Surrender is not weakness. It is trust. It means recognizing that Jesus knows us better than we know ourselves and trusting Him enough to follow when His direction is different from our own plans.

For me, this has also meant learning the difference between accepting who I am and refusing to grow. As an autistic man, I know that I experience certain parts of life differently from other people. The way I process information, communicate, respond to situations, and experience the world is part of my story. I do not need to be ashamed of that, and I do not need to pretend that I am someone I am not.

At the same time, being different does not place me beyond the call to grow. Jesus can work within the person He created while still correcting the parts of my character that do not reflect Him. I can be honest about who I am while remaining open to who He is calling me to become.

That distinction is important because transformation is not the destruction of identity. Jesus does not need to erase everything that makes us unique in order to make us more like Him. He changes what needs to be changed while teaching us to surrender our whole lives to Him.

What Does a Changed Life Look Like?

A changed life eventually becomes visible, not because we are trying to impress people, but because what Jesus changes within us begins to affect the way we live.

We begin to respond differently when we are hurt. We become more willing to forgive instead of holding onto resentment. We learn to accept correction instead of immediately defending ourselves. We become less controlled by the need for approval and more concerned with obedience to Jesus. We begin to recognize that loving Him means allowing His teachings to shape our decisions.

That does not mean we will never fail. We will still have difficult days, make mistakes, and experience moments when our emotions become stronger than our wisdom. The difference is what happens afterward.

When I fail, I do not want to run away from Jesus. I want to run toward Him.

Grace does not tell us that sin does not matter. Grace gives us a way back when we have fallen. It allows us to repent without believing that failure has permanently separated us from God. Jesus corrects us because He loves us, and His correction is part of the transformation He is producing in us.

A CHANGED RESPONSE

Come Back to Jesus.

When you fail, do not hide from Him. Bring the failure into His presence, acknowledge the truth, receive His grace, and allow Him to teach you what needs to change.

This is why transformation cannot be measured by perfection. If perfection were the evidence, none of us could claim to have been changed. The evidence is found in a heart that is increasingly willing to surrender, repent, learn, obey, and follow Jesus.

Sometimes that change happens slowly. Sometimes it happens through a single decision that nobody else notices. A person chooses forgiveness instead of bitterness, admits that they were wrong instead of defending themselves, or finally says, "Jesus, I trust You with this."

Those moments may look small to everyone else, but they matter because transformation often begins in places that nobody else can see.

Changed Does Not Mean Finished.

One of the most important things I have learned is that being changed by Jesus does not mean that the work is finished. It means that I have become willing to let Him continue working in me.

There are still areas of my life where I need to grow, things I need to surrender, and moments when I have to choose obedience over what feels easier. My journey with Jesus has not removed the need for growth. It has given me a reason to keep growing.

That gives me hope because I do not have to pretend that I have arrived. I can be honest about where I am while trusting Jesus with where He is taking me.

You may look at your own life and see areas that need to change. You may see mistakes, habits, relationships, attitudes, or decisions that you wish were different. Do not allow that realization to convince you that Jesus has abandoned you. Let it draw you closer to Him.

Transformation begins when we stop hiding and start surrendering. It begins when we allow Jesus to tell us the truth about ourselves and trust Him enough to respond. It begins when we understand that His goal is not to make us impressive, but to make us more like Him.

THE FOUNDATION

"Therefore if any person is in Christ, he is a new creation; the old things have passed away. Behold, the fresh and new has come."

2 Corinthians 5:17

Being changed is not about becoming someone else. It is about surrendering who we are to Jesus and allowing Him to transform what needs to be transformed.

The work begins in the heart, but it does not remain there. What Jesus changes within us eventually becomes visible in the way we live.

That is where our journey begins: not with the question of how different we are, but with the willingness to let Jesus change us.
