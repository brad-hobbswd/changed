CHAPTER ONE

Changed

What does it actually mean to be changed by Jesus?

"Therefore if any person is in Christ, he is a new creation."

The word changed sounds simple, but following Jesus has taught me that genuine change is much deeper than changing the things people can see. A person can change habits, circumstances, appearance, relationships, or routines and still remain the same within. Jesus calls us to something deeper. He changes the heart.

That is where this journey begins.

When I first began asking whether Jesus had truly changed me, I expected the answer to be found in the things I could point to. I could look at what I had learned, what I had experienced, what I had overcome, and how my life had developed. But the more honestly I examined myself, the more I realized that transformation could not be measured simply by what had happened around me.

The real question was what was happening within me.

Jesus can change the way we see ourselves, but He also changes the way we see other people. He can change what we value, what we pursue, what we tolerate, and what we are willing to surrender. He can change the way we respond when we are disappointed, corrected, rejected, or hurt.

That kind of change cannot be manufactured.

Jesus Changes the Heart

We often want change to happen quickly. We want the struggle to disappear, the answer to come, and the situation to improve. Sometimes God does change our circumstances, but there are other times when He begins by changing us in the middle of them.

That kind of work can be difficult because it requires honesty.

Jesus does not ask us to hide our weaknesses from Him. He already knows them. He does not ask us to pretend that we have everything under control. He knows when we do not. Instead, He invites us to surrender what we have been trying to carry ourselves.

Surrender means giving Jesus authority over the areas of our lives we would rather manage on our own. It means allowing Him to correct us when we are wrong, teach us when we do not understand, and lead us when we would rather choose the direction ourselves.

That is where change becomes personal.

THE HEART OF CHANGE

Not Appearance. Transformation.

A changed life is not simply a life that looks different from the outside. It is a life in which Jesus is transforming what is happening within.

We can learn how to speak differently without becoming more truthful. We can change our behavior without changing our motives. We can become more religious without becoming more surrendered.

Jesus is interested in something deeper.

He wants our hearts.

When the heart begins to change, the rest of life eventually follows. Our words begin to reflect what is happening inside us. Our choices begin to reveal what we value. Our relationships begin to show whether we are learning to love as Jesus taught us.

This does not mean we become perfect.

I am not perfect, and I am not writing this chapter as someone who has reached the end of the journey. I am still learning what it means to surrender. I still have moments when I respond poorly, make mistakes, or realize that an area of my life needs correction.

But I have learned that failure does not mean transformation has stopped.

Sometimes failure reveals where transformation is still needed.

Changed Does Not Mean Perfect

One of the greatest misunderstandings about following Jesus is the idea that a changed person will never struggle again. That is not the life I know.

Being changed does not mean that temptation disappears. It does not mean that difficult emotions disappear. It does not mean that we will never make another mistake or experience another difficult season.

It means that Jesus becomes the One we return to.

When we fail, we can repent. When we are weak, we can ask for strength. When we are confused, we can seek His direction. When we recognize something in ourselves that does not reflect Him, we can surrender it rather than defend it.

Transformation is not proven by never falling.

It is revealed by what we do when we fall.

Do we become defensive, or do we become humble?

Do we hide, or do we return to Jesus?

Do we blame everyone else, or are we willing to examine ourselves?

Those questions matter because following Jesus is not about protecting an image. It is about becoming willing to live honestly before God.

A CHANGED LIFE

The Direction Matters

You do not have to be perfect to be changed. You do have to be willing to follow Jesus.

The evidence of transformation is not that we never struggle. It is that our lives are increasingly being directed toward Him.

There will be days when progress feels obvious and days when it feels almost invisible. There may be areas where Jesus changes us quickly and others where surrender takes much longer.

That does not mean He has abandoned the work.

Growth takes time, but genuine growth has direction.

What Is Jesus Changing In Me?

That question is more useful than simply asking whether we are different.

Is Jesus changing the way I respond to people?

Is He changing the way I handle correction?

Is He teaching me to forgive?

Is He teaching me to trust Him when I cannot control the outcome?

Is He changing what I value?

Is He teaching me to surrender what I once refused to release?

These questions bring transformation out of the abstract and into everyday life.

Because being changed by Jesus is not something that belongs only inside a church building or during a moment of worship. It reaches into conversations, relationships, decisions, disappointments, responsibilities, and the ordinary moments nobody else sees.

That is where our faith becomes visible.

Not because we are trying to impress people, but because what Jesus does within us eventually begins to affect how we live.

The Work Is Still Happening

There are parts of my life where I can see how far Jesus has brought me, and there are other parts where I know He is still working.

I have learned not to be ashamed of that process.

I do not have to pretend that I have arrived. I do not have to hide the areas where I am still growing. I simply need to remain willing to let Jesus continue His work.

That willingness matters.

A surrendered life says, "Jesus, show me what needs to change, and help me trust You enough to change it."

That prayer can be uncomfortable, but it is also freeing. It takes away the pressure to create our own transformation and places the work where it belongs.

With Jesus.

A SIMPLE PRAYER

Jesus, Change Me.

Show me the places in my life that do not reflect You. Give me the courage to surrender them, the humility to receive Your correction, and the faith to trust You with the process.

That is the beginning of a changed life.

Not pretending.

Not performing.

Not trying to become someone else.

Surrendering.

Following.

Trusting.

Allowing Jesus to work.

The question that opened this book still matters, but perhaps it sounds different now.

Has Jesus truly changed me?

The answer will not be found simply in what I say. It will be revealed in the direction of my life, in the condition of my heart, and in my willingness to surrender myself to Jesus.

I may still struggle.

I may still fail.

I may still have much to learn.

But I do not want to remain where I was.

I want Jesus to keep changing me.

And that is what this journey is about.

THE FOUNDATION

"Therefore if any person is engrafted in Christ (the Messiah) he is a new creation (a new creature altogether); the old [previous moral and spiritual condition] has passed away. Behold, the fresh and new has come!"

2 Corinthians 5:17 (AMPC)

Jesus did not call me to become someone else. He called me to surrender my life to Him and allow Him to make me new.
