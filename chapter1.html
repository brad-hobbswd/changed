CHAPTER ONE

Changed

Discovering the difference between changing our behavior and allowing Jesus Christ to transform our hearts.

"Jesus did not come to make us a better version of ourselves. He came to make us new."

There is a remarkable difference between changing your behavior and experiencing a transformed heart. Every day, people make resolutions to become better. They promise themselves that they will be more patient, more disciplined, more forgiving, or more compassionate. While those desires may be sincere, they often depend entirely upon human strength, and human strength eventually reaches its limit. No matter how determined we may be, we cannot change the deepest parts of who we are without the transforming work of God.

The gospel presents a completely different picture of change. Jesus did not come into the world to offer a list of improvements or to teach people how to become a better version of themselves. He came to do what no person could ever accomplish through self effort. He came to give spiritual life to those who were dead in sin, to reconcile humanity to God, and to create something entirely new within those who place their faith in Him.

When I began to understand this truth, I realized that I had spent much of my life looking at change from the wrong perspective. Like many people, I believed that if I worked harder, understood more, or somehow learned how to fit in, life would become easier. I thought the answer was found in becoming more acceptable to others. Yet every attempt to gain acceptance eventually left me with the same question that had followed me for years.

Why do I still feel different?

For a long time, I believed that question needed an answer.

Today, I believe it needed a Savior.

As someone who lives with autism, I have often experienced the world differently than many people around me. I have sometimes struggled to understand why people say one thing while intending something entirely different. I have found myself confused by conversations that seemed clear to everyone else but left me wondering what I had missed. There were moments when honesty was misunderstood, sincerity was questioned, and my desire to do what was right was interpreted in ways I never intended.

Those experiences were often painful, but they also taught me something valuable.

Feeling different is not the same as being changed.

One describes an experience. The other describes a work of God.

When Jesus Christ entered my life, He did not erase my personality or ask me to become someone I was never created to be. Instead, He began transforming my heart. He changed my desires, my purpose, and the direction of my life. The greatest miracle was not that I suddenly understood everything around me. The greatest miracle was that I began to understand the One who had always understood me.

That realization changed everything.

The world often measures a person's value by how well they fit within its expectations. If you think like everyone else, speak like everyone else, and pursue the same goals as everyone else, you are usually accepted without much resistance. Following Jesus, however, leads us in another direction. The closer we walk with Him, the more our values, priorities, and convictions begin to reflect His character rather than the culture around us.

THE DIFFERENCE

Popularity or Discipleship?

Jesus never promised that following Him would make us popular. He promised that it would make us His disciples. There is a profound difference between those two realities. Popularity seeks the approval of people, but discipleship seeks the approval of God. One changes according to public opinion, while the other remains anchored in eternal truth.

As I continued growing in my relationship with Christ, I discovered that many of the moments when I felt alone were not signs that God had abandoned me. Instead, they became opportunities to trust Him more deeply. Standing apart from the crowd was no longer simply a consequence of feeling different. It became a choice to remain faithful to Christ, even when that faithfulness was misunderstood.

Throughout Scripture, God repeatedly called ordinary men and women to live differently because they had first been changed. Noah continued building the ark while everyone around him mocked what they could not understand. Abraham left everything familiar because he trusted the voice of God more than the comfort of certainty. Daniel refused to compromise his devotion, even when obedience placed his own life at risk. The apostles continued proclaiming the name of Jesus despite persecution because they knew that the One who had changed them was worthy of every sacrifice.

None of these people were seeking attention.

They were seeking God.

Their lives became different because their hearts had already been changed.

That same invitation is extended to every believer today. Jesus does not call us to imitate the crowd or to measure our faith by the standards of a changing culture. He calls us to deny ourselves, take up our cross, and follow Him. That calling will not always be easy, and it will not always be understood by those around us, but it will always be worth it because the One who calls us is faithful.

Perhaps you have spent years feeling like you never quite fit in. Perhaps you have questioned why your journey has seemed different from the journeys of those around you. Maybe you have wondered whether God could use your unique experiences for His glory.

I want you to know that He can.

The Lord has never been limited by what makes us feel different. Throughout history, He has demonstrated His power by working through people whom the world underestimated, overlooked, or misunderstood. God's strength is revealed most clearly when we stop trying to become who the world expects us to be and begin surrendering ourselves to who He is calling us to become.

The purpose of this book is not to convince you that being different is enough.

It is to remind you that being changed changes everything.

THE HEART OF THIS CHAPTER

Changed Changes Everything

When Jesus transforms a heart, He transforms a life. That transformation may not remove every struggle or answer every question, but it gives us something far greater. It gives us a new identity, a living hope, and the assurance that we belong to Christ. Once that truth takes root within us, we no longer spend our lives searching for acceptance from the world because we have already been accepted by the One who gave His life to redeem us.

As you continue reading this book, my prayer is that you will begin to see your own story through the lens of God's grace. Whatever your past has been, whatever challenges you face today, and whatever makes you feel different from those around you, remember this one truth.

Jesus Christ is still changing lives.

A life that has truly been changed can never remain the same.

SCRIPTURE

2 Corinthians 5:17
