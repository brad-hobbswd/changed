CHAPTER FIVE

The Evidence of a Changed Life

When Jesus changes the heart, that change eventually becomes visible in the way we live, love, respond, forgive, and follow Him.

"The evidence that Jesus has changed me is not that I have become someone else. It is that the person He created me to be is becoming more surrendered to Him."

Every tree tells a story. Long before anyone examines its roots, they can look at its fruit and know what kind of tree it is. An apple tree does not struggle to prove that it is an apple tree, because its fruit eventually reveals what is growing within it. In much the same way, a life that has truly been touched and transformed by Jesus Christ will eventually reveal that transformation through the way a person lives.

Jesus did not call us simply to say that we belong to Him. He called us to follow Him. That means our faith cannot remain only in our words, because what is happening within the heart will eventually influence what comes out of the life. Our choices, our relationships, our responses, our priorities, and even the way we handle difficult moments can become evidence of the work Jesus is doing within us.

This does not mean that a changed life becomes a perfect life. I am not perfect, and I do not believe any honest follower of Jesus should pretend to be. Being changed does not mean that I never become frustrated, overwhelmed, hurt, confused, or disappointed. It means that I am learning to bring those things to Jesus instead of allowing them to become the master of my life.

SCRIPTURE

"Even so every good tree bringeth forth good fruit; but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit."

Matthew 7:17

That distinction is especially important for me because I know what it is like to experience the world differently from many of the people around me. As an autistic person, there have been moments when people have misunderstood my words, my reactions, my expressions, or the way I process situations. There have been times when something happening inside of me was completely different from what someone else assumed they were seeing on the outside.

Autism is not something that makes me spiritually broken, and it is not something Jesus needs to remove in order for me to have purpose. God does not require me to become someone else before I can belong to Him. He knows how He created me, and He knows the ways I experience the world. My autism is part of my story, but it is not the foundation of my identity. My identity is found in Jesus Christ.

At the same time, being autistic does not mean that I am beyond the need for spiritual growth. Just as every other believer must allow Jesus to work within the heart, I must allow Him to challenge my responses, my attitudes, my fears, my frustrations, and the places where I am tempted to protect myself instead of trusting Him. The goal is not to become less autistic. The goal is to become more like Jesus.

I do not need to hide who I am.

I need to surrender who I am to Jesus.

There is a difference between authenticity and refusing to change. Being authentic means I do not have to pretend to be someone I am not. Surrender means I allow Jesus to show me where my character, my reactions, and my choices still need to grow. Those two things can exist together. I can embrace the person God created me to be while allowing Jesus Christ to transform everything within me that does not reflect Him.

That is where the message behind Different by Micah Tyler became meaningful to my own journey. The song challenged me to think about what it means for the presence of Jesus to become visible in a person's life. It made me consider whether I was simply different from other people or whether Jesus was actually changing me. Those are not the same thing.

I can be different because of my personality. I can be different because I am autistic. I can be different because of my experiences, my perspective, or the way I process the world. None of those things automatically mean that I am spiritually transformed. The question is deeper than being different.

What is Jesus producing in me?

That question changed the way I began looking at my own life. Instead of constantly asking whether people understood me, I began asking whether I was allowing Jesus to teach me how to respond when they did not. Instead of asking why someone had misunderstood me, I began asking whether I could still respond with grace. Instead of allowing rejection to determine my identity, I began learning to remember that my identity was already secure in Christ.

"Fruit is never the root of salvation. It is the evidence that something alive is growing within us."

A changed heart eventually produces changed behavior because what fills the heart eventually becomes visible in the life. This does not happen because we manufacture holiness through our own strength. It happens because Jesus is working within us, and His Spirit is teaching us how to live differently.

SCRIPTURE

"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance..."

Galatians 5:22–23

Love begins replacing hatred. Joy begins appearing even when circumstances are difficult. Peace begins taking root where anxiety and uncertainty once controlled our thoughts. Patience begins growing where frustration once ruled our responses. Kindness becomes more than something we show when it is convenient. Goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self control begin becoming visible because Christ is continuing His work within us.

That does not mean those qualities appear perfectly every day. There are still days when I fall short. There are still moments when I react before I think, become frustrated because I feel misunderstood, or struggle to process what is happening around me. There are moments when I need to step back, pray, breathe, and allow Jesus to regain the place that my emotions have tried to take.

That is not failure. That is part of learning to walk with Christ.

The evidence of change is not that I never struggle. The evidence is that I no longer want to remain where I was. I want Jesus to continue working within me. I want Him to correct me when I am wrong, strengthen me when I am weak, comfort me when I am hurting, and teach me when I do not understand.

REFLECTION

What Kind Of Fruit Are You Producing?

When people encounter you, do they simply see your personality, your circumstances, your differences, and your struggles, or do they also see evidence that Jesus Christ is changing your heart?

There have been seasons in my own life when I wanted people to understand my intentions more than I wanted them to see Christ in me. I wanted misunderstandings to disappear. I wanted people to know what I meant instead of what they assumed I meant. I wanted every false accusation corrected and every unfair judgment answered.

But eventually I had to ask a harder question.

"What kind of fruit am I producing while I am waiting to be understood?"

That question humbled me because it reminded me that I cannot control what another person thinks about me. I cannot make everyone understand autism. I cannot force people to understand my intentions. I cannot make everyone accept me. What I can do is surrender my response to Jesus Christ.

I can choose patience when I want to become frustrated. I can choose forgiveness when I want to hold onto resentment. I can choose humility when I want to prove myself right. I can choose truth without becoming cruel. I can choose boundaries without becoming bitter. I can choose to walk away from an argument without believing that walking away means I have lost.

Those choices do not erase my autism, and they do not require me to pretend that difficult situations are easy. They demonstrate something deeper. They show that Jesus is teaching me how to live through those situations without allowing them to control who I become.

The Difference Should Point To Jesus

The world already knows that people are different from one another. We have different personalities, different experiences, different abilities, different ways of thinking, and different stories. Being different by itself is not the evidence that Jesus has changed us.

The greater evidence is what Jesus does with those differences.

He can take the person who has felt misunderstood and teach him compassion for people who are also misunderstood. He can take the person who has experienced rejection and teach him how to love without requiring everyone else's approval. He can take painful experiences and use them to develop patience, wisdom, humility, and mercy.

That is what I want my life to demonstrate.

I do not want people to look at me and simply see someone who is different. I want them to see someone who belongs to Jesus Christ. I do not want my autism to become the only thing people remember about me. I want my life to show that an autistic man can love Jesus deeply, follow Him faithfully, struggle honestly, grow continually, and become more like Christ without pretending to be someone else.

I want the difference in my life to point beyond me.

I want people to see Jesus.

SCRIPTURE

"Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me."

John 15:4

A branch does not produce fruit by struggling to look like another branch. It produces fruit by remaining connected to the vine. That is an important picture of the Christian life because I cannot transform myself by simply trying harder to become the person I think I should be.

I need Jesus.

I need His presence. I need His Word. I need His grace. I need His correction. I need His forgiveness. I need His strength when I do not have enough strength of my own. I need Him when I understand what is happening, and I need Him when I do not understand it at all.

The more I remain connected to Him, the more His character begins to become visible in my life.

Changed Does Not Mean Finished

Sometimes we think that if Jesus has truly changed us, we should already have everything figured out. We assume that a changed person should never struggle with the same weakness twice, never become overwhelmed, never make a mistake, and never need to apologize.

That is not transformation. That is an impossible standard.

Changed means I am no longer satisfied with remaining the same. It means I am willing to let Jesus continue working in me. It means I can recognize when something within me does not reflect Him and bring it honestly before Him. It means I can repent without believing repentance makes me worthless.

I know that I am far from perfect, but the cross reminds me that my imperfections do not disqualify me from the love of Jesus. His grace does not tell me that I am finished. His grace tells me that He is not finished with me.

That is why I can keep going.

I can keep learning.

I can keep surrendering.

I can keep becoming.

THE EVIDENCE

Let Them See Jesus In Me

The greatest evidence of a changed life is not perfection. It is surrender. It is a heart that continually turns toward Jesus and allows Him to shape the person within.

One day, someone may look at your life and wonder what made the difference. They may see the way you respond to difficulty, the way you forgive, the way you love people who misunderstand you, or the way you continue trusting God when circumstances do not make sense.

They may wonder how you can continue standing when life has given you every reason to quit.

The answer should not ultimately be your personality, your determination, your strength, or your ability to overcome.

The answer should be Jesus Christ.

When He changes the root, the fruit begins to change as well. When He changes the heart, the life begins to reveal it. When He becomes the center of who we are, everything else begins to find its proper place.

That is the kind of different I want to be.

Not different simply because I am autistic.

Not different simply because I think differently.

Not different simply because I have experienced things other people may never understand.

I want to be different because Jesus Christ is changing me.

I want His love to be seen in me. I want His patience to be heard in my words. I want His compassion to be recognized in my actions. I want His forgiveness to be evident when I have every reason to hold onto resentment. I want His truth to guide me when compromise would be easier.

Most of all, when people see me, I want them to see more than Bradley.

I want them to see Jesus.

SCRIPTURE

"And being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ."

Philippians 1:6

I Want To Be Changed

I do not want to spend my life trapped in the same patterns, controlled by the same fears, defined by old wounds, or consumed by the need to prove myself to people who may never understand me. I do not want to gain the approval of the world while losing the things that matter most to God.

I want Jesus Christ to have every part of me.

I want Him to change my heart before He changes my circumstances. I want Him to change my responses before He changes the people around me. I want Him to change my desires before He gives me everything I think I need. I want Him to finish the work He started in me.

I want to be different.

I want to be changed.

Not so that people will simply notice me.

But so that when people notice something different about me, they will ultimately see Him.

Because the greatest evidence that Jesus has changed my life is not that I have become someone else.

It is that I am becoming more like Him.

THE JOURNEY CONTINUES

"Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ."

Philippians 1:6
