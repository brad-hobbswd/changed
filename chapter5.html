CHAPTER FIVE

The Evidence of a Changed Life

When Jesus changes the heart, that transformation eventually becomes visible in the way we live, love, respond, forgive, and follow Him.

"The evidence that Jesus has changed me is not that I have become someone else. It is that I am becoming more like Him."

A changed heart eventually produces a changed life. We may not notice everything happening beneath the surface, but what is growing within us eventually becomes visible through our words, choices, relationships, and responses. Jesus did not call us merely to say that we belong to Him. He called us to follow Him, and following Him should begin to shape the way we live.

This does not mean that a changed life becomes a perfect life. I am not perfect, and I do not believe transformation means that I will never struggle, become frustrated, feel overwhelmed, or make mistakes. Being changed means that I am learning to bring those things to Jesus rather than allowing them to control me.

The evidence of transformation is not perfection. It is direction.

When Jesus is changing us, the direction of our lives begins to move toward Him.

SCRIPTURE

"Even so every good tree bringeth forth good fruit; but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit."

Matthew 7:17

Jesus used the picture of a tree and its fruit to help us understand this principle. A tree does not have to announce what kind of tree it is. Eventually, its fruit reveals what is growing within it.

The same principle applies to our lives.

What is happening within the heart eventually becomes visible. Our attitudes begin to show it. Our relationships begin to show it. Our words begin to show it. Even the way we respond during difficult seasons can reveal whether we are allowing Jesus to shape us.

What Is Growing Within Me?

That question has challenged me personally. As an autistic person, I know what it is like to experience situations differently from the people around me. There have been times when my words, expressions, reactions, or responses were misunderstood because people could not see what was happening beneath the surface.

My autism is part of my story, but it is not the foundation of my identity. Jesus knows how He created me, and I do not need to become someone else to belong to Him. At the same time, being different does not remove my responsibility to grow.

Jesus can teach me how to respond when I am overwhelmed. He can teach me how to communicate when I feel misunderstood. He can teach me how to forgive when I have been hurt and how to remain patient when I would rather react.

The goal is not to become less of who God created me to be.

The goal is to become more like Jesus.

THE EVIDENCE

A Changed Heart Produces Changed Fruit

Transformation becomes visible through the character we develop, the choices we make, and the way we treat people when life becomes difficult.

That fruit does not appear because we simply try harder to look Christian. It grows as we remain connected to Jesus and allow Him to work within us.

THE FRUIT OF THE SPIRIT

"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance..."

Galatians 5:22–23

Love begins to replace hatred. Patience begins to replace frustration. Gentleness begins to replace harshness. Humility begins to replace pride. Forgiveness begins to replace bitterness. Faith begins to replace fear. Self control begins to replace the need to react according to every emotion we experience.

None of these things appear perfectly every day. We are still growing, and there will be moments when we fall short. What matters is that we continue allowing Jesus to work.

Sometimes the evidence of change is found in something very simple. Maybe I used to react immediately, but now I pause. Maybe I once held onto anger for days, but now I recognize it sooner. Maybe I once refused correction, but now I can admit when I am wrong. Maybe I used to focus on being understood, but now I am learning to understand others.

Those changes may seem small, but they matter.

Transformation Becomes Visible

There have been times when I wanted people to understand my intentions more than I wanted them to see Jesus in my response. I wanted every misunderstanding corrected. I wanted people to know what I meant and why I responded the way I did.

But eventually I had to ask myself a harder question.

"What kind of fruit am I producing while I am waiting to be understood?"

That question changed the way I looked at difficult situations. I cannot control what another person thinks about me. I cannot make everyone understand my experiences. I cannot force everyone to accept me.

But I can surrender my response to Jesus.

I can choose patience without pretending that I was not hurt. I can choose forgiveness without pretending that nothing happened. I can speak truth without becoming cruel. I can establish boundaries without becoming bitter. I can walk away from an argument without believing that I have lost.

Those choices become evidence.

They show that Jesus is teaching me how to live through difficult situations without allowing those situations to determine who I become.

PERSONAL EXAMINATION

What Does My Life Reveal?

When I am frustrated, what comes out of me?

When I am corrected, am I willing to listen?

When I am hurt, do I move toward forgiveness?

When I am misunderstood, do I respond with grace?

When I am afraid, do I trust Jesus?

When nobody is watching, does my life still reflect what I say I believe?

These questions are not meant to condemn us. They are meant to help us examine our lives honestly.

If we discover something that does not reflect Jesus, we do not have to hide from Him. We can bring it to Him.

That is where grace meets transformation.

Changed Does Not Mean Finished.

One of the greatest mistakes we can make is believing that transformation means we should already have everything figured out. A changed person can still struggle. A changed person can still need correction. A changed person can still apologize, repent, and begin again.

Being changed means that we are no longer satisfied with remaining the same.

I know there are still areas of my life where Jesus is working. There are still moments when I need to slow down, listen, apologize, forgive, and surrender. There are still things I am learning about myself and about what it means to follow Jesus faithfully.

But I do not want those unfinished places to discourage me.

Jesus is not finished with me.

The evidence of His work is not that I have reached perfection. It is that I keep returning to Him and allowing Him to continue changing me.

SCRIPTURE

"Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me."

John 15:4

A branch does not produce fruit by trying to become another branch. It produces fruit by remaining connected to the vine.

That picture reminds me that I cannot transform myself through determination alone. I need Jesus.

I need His Word. I need His presence. I need His grace. I need His correction. I need His forgiveness. I need His strength when my own strength is not enough.

The closer I remain to Him, the more His character can become visible in my life.

The Difference Should Point to Jesus.

People already know that human beings are different. We have different personalities, experiences, abilities, perspectives, and stories. Being different by itself is not evidence that Jesus has changed us.

The greater evidence is what Jesus produces through our lives.

He can take rejection and teach us compassion. He can take misunderstanding and teach us patience. He can take failure and teach us humility. He can take pain and teach us mercy.

That is what I want my life to demonstrate.

I do not want people to look at me and see only my differences. I want them to see someone who belongs to Jesus Christ. I want my autism to be understood as part of my story without becoming the only story people remember.

I want people to see an autistic man who loves Jesus, follows Him, struggles honestly, grows continually, and refuses to let his differences become an excuse for disobedience.

Most of all, I want my life to point beyond me.

Let Them See Jesus.

That is the evidence I want.

When I am patient, let them see Jesus.

When I forgive, let them see Jesus.

When I serve, let them see Jesus.

When I admit that I am wrong, let them see Jesus.

When I stand for truth with love, let them see Jesus.

When I fail and return to Him in repentance, let them see Jesus.

I do not want people to praise me for being strong enough to overcome everything.

I want them to recognize that I have a Savior who continues to work in me.

THE PURPOSE OF A CHANGED LIFE

Make Jesus Visible

The purpose of transformation is not to make ourselves impressive. It is to allow the character of Jesus Christ to become visible through the way we live.

That is why I no longer want to measure my life only by how different I am from other people. I want to measure it by whether I am becoming more like Jesus.

Am I becoming more loving?

Am I becoming more patient?

Am I becoming more forgiving?

Am I becoming more humble?

Am I becoming more willing to obey?

Am I becoming more dependent upon Jesus?

Those questions matter far more than whether I fit someone else's expectations.

I Want to Keep Growing.

I do not want to spend my life trapped in the same patterns, controlled by the same fears, or defined by old wounds. I do not want to spend my Christian life talking about Jesus without allowing Him to change me.

I want my life to tell the truth about what my mouth says I believe.

I want Jesus to change my heart before He changes my circumstances. I want Him to change my responses before He changes the people around me. I want Him to change my desires and teach me to trust His will above my own.

I want to be different because Jesus has changed me.

Not different simply because I am autistic.

Not different simply because I think differently.

Not different simply because my experiences have shaped me differently.

I want to be different because Jesus Christ is changing me.

THE WORK CONTINUES

"Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ."

Philippians 1:6

The evidence of a changed life is not perfection.

It is surrender.

It is growth.

It is a heart that keeps turning toward Jesus.

When Jesus changes the root, the fruit begins to change. When He changes the heart, the life begins to reveal it. And when people see that change in us, may they see more than us. May they see Jesus.
