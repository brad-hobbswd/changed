CHAPTER FOUR

The Battle Within

Following Jesus does not remove every struggle. It teaches us how to surrender those struggles to Him and allow His Spirit to change the way we live.

Chapter Four • Learning to Overcome Within
"The greatest battles are often fought in places nobody else can see."

There is a battle that takes place inside every believer. We can know what is right and still struggle to choose it, love Jesus and still wrestle with attitudes or desires that do not reflect Him, and genuinely want to follow Him while discovering that some of our old responses are not surrendered as completely as we thought. That struggle does not mean Jesus has abandoned us. It reminds us that transformation is still taking place.

Following Jesus does not mean that temptation disappears or that every weakness is immediately removed. It means that we no longer have to face those struggles alone. Jesus gives us the grace to recognize what is happening within us, the wisdom to respond differently, and the strength to continue surrendering ourselves to Him.

The Battle Begins Within.

It is easy to believe that our greatest problems are outside of us. Difficult circumstances, rejection, disappointment, misunderstandings, and difficult people can all affect us deeply, and sometimes other people genuinely do hurt us. Yet even when circumstances are beyond our control, our response remains something we must bring before Jesus. Someone may make me angry, but I am responsible for what I do with that anger. Someone may misunderstand me, but I still have to decide whether I will respond with grace or bitterness. Someone may hurt me, but I must choose whether I will allow that pain to control the way I treat everyone else.

THE BATTLE WITHIN

I Cannot Control Everything Around Me.

But I can surrender my response to Jesus. Transformation begins when I stop allowing circumstances to determine the condition of my heart.

I have learned that being overwhelmed, misunderstood, or frustrated can make it difficult to respond the way I want to respond. As an autistic person, I know that certain situations can require additional processing, clearer communication, or more time before I know how I want to respond. That reality deserves understanding, but it does not remove the opportunity to grow. Jesus is not asking me to pretend that something is easy when it is not. He is teaching me what to do with the difficulty.

Sometimes that means pausing before I respond, praying for wisdom, asking for clarification, or stepping away long enough to regain composure. If I respond wrongly, it means taking responsibility instead of using the difficulty as an excuse. I can repent, learn from what happened, and try again. That is part of growth.

The Battle Is Not Proof That I Have Failed.

There have been times when I have looked at my own struggles and wondered why I still deal with certain things. I have questioned whether I should be further along by now and wondered why something I thought I had surrendered seemed to return when another difficult situation came along. But spiritual growth is not measured by never struggling again. Sometimes growth is revealed by how quickly we recognize the struggle and turn toward Jesus.

Perhaps I once reacted immediately, but now I am learning to pause. Perhaps I once held onto anger for days, but now I recognize it sooner. Perhaps I once refused correction, but now I can admit when I am wrong. Perhaps I once avoided difficult conversations, but now I can approach them with prayer and humility. The change may not always feel dramatic, but that does not mean it is not real.

THE INNER BATTLE

"For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary the one to the other."

Galatians 5:17

The battle between our flesh and the Spirit is something we should recognize rather than ignore. When anger rises, pride wants to take control, fear begins directing our decisions, or our own desires become more important than obedience, we are given another opportunity to surrender. The question is not whether the battle will ever come. The question is who will lead us when it does.

Who Is Going to Lead Me?

Will I allow my emotions to lead me, or will I allow Jesus to lead me? Will fear determine my decisions, or will faith? Will pride control my response, or will humility? Will the opinions of other people become the authority over my identity, or will I remember who I am in Jesus Christ? These decisions may have to be made more than once in a single day, sometimes in the middle of a difficult conversation and sometimes when nobody else knows what is happening inside us.

That is why surrender cannot remain an idea. It has to become a practice.

Learning to Pause

One of the most practical ways I have learned to face the battle within is to stop before reacting. That pause creates space between what happens to me and what I choose to do next. In that space, I can pray, ask Jesus for wisdom, and consider whether my response reflects Him. I can remember that being right does not give me permission to be cruel, being hurt does not give me permission to hurt someone else, and being misunderstood does not require me to prove myself to everyone.

A PERSONAL EXAMINATION

What Controls My Response?

When I am frustrated, what comes out of me? When I am corrected, do I become defensive or teachable? When I am hurt, do I seek healing or revenge? When I am afraid, do I trust Jesus or immediately take control? When I am misunderstood, do I respond with grace or bitterness?

These questions are not meant to shame me. They help me recognize where the battle is happening. I cannot surrender what I refuse to see, which is why honesty is so important. Jesus already knows what is happening inside me, so I do not have to hide the anger, fear, confusion, frustration, insecurity, or weakness that I would rather nobody else see. I can bring those things to Him and ask Him to teach me what to do with them.

Grace Meets Me in the Battle.

There have been times when I have failed in the middle of the battle. I have responded poorly, allowed frustration to speak louder than wisdom, allowed fear to influence my decisions, or said things I later wished I had said differently. When that happens, I have a choice. I can defend myself and pretend nothing happened, or I can humble myself, acknowledge the truth, and bring the failure to Jesus.

Repentance is part of transformation because it means I stop making excuses and allow Jesus to show me what went wrong. It gives me the opportunity to learn a different response instead of remaining trapped in the same pattern. Grace does not tell me that my actions do not matter. Grace gives me a way forward when I have fallen short.

I can repent, receive forgiveness, learn from what happened, and continue following Jesus. Failure does not have to become my identity. It can become instruction when I allow Jesus to use it to teach me.

The Battle Does Not Define Me.

My struggles are real, but they are not my identity. My weaknesses are real, but they are not my identity. My mistakes are real, but they are not my identity. Even the things I am still learning do not determine the final story of my life. Jesus does.

That truth matters because it keeps me from becoming discouraged when I recognize how much growth is still needed. I do not have to become hopeless because I am not finished. None of us are finished. The goal is not to reach a place where we never need Jesus again. The goal is to become increasingly dependent upon Him.

REMEMBER

The Battle Is an Invitation to Surrender.

When you recognize a struggle within yourself, do not run from Jesus. Bring it to Him. The place where you feel weakest may become the place where you learn to depend upon Him most.

There are still areas where I am learning. I am learning to respond instead of react, to listen before defending myself, to forgive without keeping score, and to accept correction without automatically interpreting it as rejection. I am learning to trust Jesus when I cannot control the outcome and to understand that I do not have to win every argument in order to remain faithful to Him.

Most importantly, I am learning that I do not have to fight the battle within by myself.

Jesus Is With Me in the Battle.

The Christian life is not a promise that we will never struggle. It is a promise that Jesus will never abandon us in the struggle. When I am frustrated, afraid, tempted, or discouraged, He is still there. When I fail and need correction, He is still there. When I need strength to choose obedience, He is still there.

That gives me hope because I do not have to win every battle through my own strength. I have to keep bringing the battle to Jesus and remain willing to surrender what He reveals.

THE PROMISE

"Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ."

Philippians 1:6

I Am Still Becoming.

That is the truth I want to remember when I become frustrated with myself. I am still becoming, and Jesus has not finished His work. There will be more moments when I need to choose patience, more opportunities to forgive, more situations where I have to surrender control, and more times when I must choose faith over fear and obedience over comfort.

The battle within may continue, but I do not have to fear it. Every struggle can become another opportunity to surrender, every failure can become another opportunity to repent, every difficult situation can become another opportunity to trust, and every victory can become another reason to give Jesus the glory.

I do not want to become discouraged because I am still fighting. I want to become encouraged because Jesus is still working. The battle within is not proof that Jesus has stopped changing me. It is another place where I can surrender to Him and discover that I was never fighting alone.

The battle within may continue, but so does the work of Jesus. I will keep surrendering, keep trusting, and keep allowing Him to change me from the inside out.
