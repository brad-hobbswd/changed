CHAPTER FOUR

The Battle Within

Following Jesus does not remove every struggle. It teaches us how to surrender those struggles to Him and allow His Spirit to change the way we live.

Chapter Four • Learning to Overcome Within
"The greatest battles are often fought in places nobody else can see."

There is a battle that takes place inside every believer. It is the battle between what we know we should do and what we sometimes want to do. We can know the right thing and still struggle to choose it. We can love Jesus and still wrestle with attitudes, desires, fears, and reactions that do not reflect Him.

That struggle does not mean that Jesus has abandoned us. It reminds us that transformation is still taking place.

Following Jesus does not mean that temptation disappears or that every weakness is immediately removed. It means we no longer have to face those things alone. Jesus gives us the strength to recognize what is happening within us and the grace to choose a different way.

The Battle Begins Within.

It is easy to believe that our greatest problems are outside of us. We may blame difficult circumstances, difficult people, disappointment, rejection, or the situations we cannot control. Sometimes those things really are difficult, and sometimes other people really do hurt us.

But even when circumstances are outside our control, our response is still something we must bring before Jesus.

Someone may make me angry, but I am responsible for what I do with that anger. Someone may misunderstand me, but I must decide whether I will respond with grace or bitterness. Someone may hurt me, but I must decide whether I will allow that pain to control the way I treat everyone else.

That is where the battle becomes personal.

THE BATTLE WITHIN

I Cannot Control Everything Around Me.

But I can surrender my response to Jesus. Transformation begins when I stop allowing circumstances to determine the condition of my heart.

I have learned that being overwhelmed, misunderstood, or frustrated can make it difficult to respond the way I want to respond. As an autistic person, I know that certain situations can require more processing, more clarity, or more time. That reality deserves understanding, but it does not remove the opportunity to grow.

Jesus is not asking me to pretend that something is easy when it is not. He is teaching me what to do with the difficulty.

I can pause.

I can pray.

I can ask for clarification.

I can step away when I need to regain composure.

And when I respond wrongly, I can take responsibility, repent, and try again.

That is growth.

The Battle Is Not Proof That I Have Failed.

Sometimes I have looked at my own struggles and wondered why I still deal with certain things. I have questioned whether I should be further along by now. I have wondered why something I thought I had surrendered seems to return when another difficult situation comes along.

But spiritual growth is not measured by never struggling again.

Sometimes growth is revealed by how quickly we recognize the struggle and turn toward Jesus.

Maybe I used to react immediately, but now I pause.

Maybe I used to hold onto anger for days, but now I recognize it sooner.

Maybe I once refused correction, but now I can admit when I am wrong.

Maybe I used to run from difficult conversations, but now I can approach them with prayer and humility.

The change may not always feel dramatic, but it is still change.

THE INNER BATTLE

"For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary the one to the other."

Galatians 5:17

The battle between our flesh and the Spirit is not something we should ignore. It is something we should recognize. When we feel anger rising, when pride wants to take control, when fear begins directing our decisions, or when we want to respond according to our own desires, we have an opportunity to surrender.

The question becomes simple.

Who Is Going to Lead Me?

Will I allow my emotions to lead me?

Will I allow fear to lead me?

Will I allow pride to lead me?

Will I allow the opinions of other people to lead me?

Or will I allow Jesus to lead me?

That decision may have to be made more than once in a single day.

Sometimes it must be made in the middle of a conversation. Sometimes it must be made when nobody else knows what is happening inside us. Sometimes it must be made when we are tired, frustrated, hurt, or afraid.

That is why surrender cannot remain an idea.

It has to become a practice.

Learning to Pause

One of the most practical ways I have learned to fight the battle within is to stop before reacting.

That pause creates space between what happens to me and what I choose to do next.

In that space, I can pray.

I can ask Jesus for wisdom.

I can think about whether my response reflects Him.

I can remember that being right does not give me permission to be cruel.

I can remember that being hurt does not give me permission to hurt someone else.

I can remember that being misunderstood does not require me to prove myself to everyone.

Sometimes the most spiritual thing I can do is pause and let Jesus have the first word instead of allowing my emotions to have it.

A PERSONAL EXAMINATION

What Controls My Response?

When I am frustrated, what comes out of me?

When I am corrected, do I become defensive or teachable?

When I am hurt, do I seek healing or revenge?

When I am afraid, do I trust Jesus or immediately take control?

When I am misunderstood, do I respond with grace or bitterness?

These questions are not meant to shame me. They help me recognize where the battle is happening.

I cannot surrender what I refuse to see.

That is why honesty is so important.

Jesus already knows what is happening inside me. I do not have to hide it from Him. I can bring Him the anger, fear, confusion, frustration, insecurity, and weakness that I would rather nobody else see.

Then I can ask Him to teach me what to do with it.

Grace Meets Me in the Battle.

There have been times when I have failed in the middle of the battle. I have responded poorly. I have allowed frustration to speak louder than wisdom. I have allowed fear to influence my decisions. I have said things I wish I had said differently.

When that happens, I have a choice.

I can defend myself and pretend nothing happened, or I can humble myself and bring the failure to Jesus.

Repentance is part of transformation.

It means I stop making excuses and acknowledge the truth. It means I allow Jesus to show me what went wrong and teach me how to respond differently the next time.

Failure does not have to become identity.

It can become instruction.

That is one of the reasons grace is so powerful. Grace does not tell me that my actions do not matter. Grace gives me a way forward when I have fallen short.

I can repent.

I can receive forgiveness.

I can learn.

I can get back up.

And I can keep following Jesus.

The Battle Does Not Define Me.

My struggles are real, but they are not my identity.

My weaknesses are real, but they are not my identity.

My mistakes are real, but they are not my identity.

Even the things I am still learning do not determine the final story of my life.

Jesus does.

That truth matters because it keeps me from becoming discouraged when I see how much growth is still needed. I do not have to become hopeless because I am not finished.

None of us are finished.

The goal is not to reach a place where we never need Jesus again.

The goal is to become increasingly dependent upon Him.

REMEMBER

The Battle Is an Invitation to Surrender.

When you recognize a struggle within yourself, do not run from Jesus. Bring it to Him. The place where you feel weakest may become the place where you learn to depend upon Him most.

There are still areas where I am learning.

I am learning to respond instead of react.

I am learning to listen before defending myself.

I am learning to forgive without keeping score.

I am learning to accept correction without allowing it to become rejection.

I am learning to trust Jesus when I cannot control the outcome.

I am learning that I do not have to win every argument to remain faithful to Him.

Most importantly, I am learning that I do not have to fight the battle within by myself.

Jesus Is With Me in the Battle.

The Christian life is not a promise that we will never struggle. It is a promise that Jesus will never abandon us in the struggle.

When I am frustrated, He is there.

When I am afraid, He is there.

When I am tempted, He is there.

When I fail, He is there.

When I need correction, He is there.

When I need strength to choose obedience, He is there.

That gives me hope.

I do not have to win every battle through my own strength. I have to keep bringing the battle to Jesus.

THE PROMISE

"Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ."

Philippians 1:6

I Am Still Becoming.

That is the truth I want to remember when I become frustrated with myself.

I am still becoming.

Jesus has not finished His work.

There will be more moments when I have to choose patience. There will be more opportunities to forgive. There will be more times when I need to surrender control. There will be more moments when I have to choose faith over fear and obedience over comfort.

The battle within will continue, but I do not have to fear it.

Every battle can become another opportunity to surrender.

Every failure can become another opportunity to repent.

Every difficult situation can become another opportunity to trust.

Every victory can become another reason to give Jesus the glory.

I do not want to become discouraged because I am still fighting.

I want to become encouraged because Jesus is still working.

That is the difference.

The battle within is not proof that Jesus has stopped changing me.

It is another place where I can surrender to Him.

And when I surrender, I discover that I was never fighting alone.

The battle within may continue, but so does the work of Jesus. I will keep surrendering, keep trusting, and keep allowing Him to change me from the inside out.
