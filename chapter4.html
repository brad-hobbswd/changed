CHAPTER FOUR

The Battle Within

Sometimes the greatest battle is not learning how to be different, but learning to surrender every part of who we are to Jesus Christ and allowing Him to change us from within.

"Being different is not the same thing as being changed. Jesus does not ask us to become someone else. He asks us to surrender who we are to Him."

One of the greatest things I have had to learn is that being different and being changed are not the same thing. For much of my life, I knew that I experienced the world differently from many of the people around me. As an autistic person, I have often processed conversations, relationships, emotions, expectations, and situations in ways that other people did not always understand. There were times when I wondered whether the things that made me different were something I needed to hide in order to be accepted.

But Jesus began teaching me something deeper. He was not calling me to spend my life trying to become a copy of everyone else. He was calling me to become more like Him. My autism is part of my lived experience, but it does not define my worth, my purpose, or my identity in Christ. At the same time, following Jesus does not mean pretending that my struggles, differences, or weaknesses do not exist. It means bringing every part of my life before Him and allowing Him to show me what needs to change.

That distinction matters because sometimes we confuse being different with being spiritually transformed. A person can be different from everyone around them and still carry bitterness, pride, unforgiveness, selfishness, fear, or sin within their heart. We can be misunderstood by people and still need Jesus to work deeply within us. The goal of this journey is therefore not simply to stand apart from the world. The goal is to belong completely to Jesus Christ.

I do not need to become someone else.

I need Jesus to change me.

That realization has become especially important in my own journey. There were times when I thought being accepted meant learning how to act like everyone else, hiding the things that made me uncomfortable, or remaining silent when I saw something that did not feel right. I spent time trying to understand expectations that seemed obvious to other people but were not always obvious to me. Sometimes I became exhausted trying to fit into environments where I felt like I was constantly being measured against a standard I could not fully understand.

Yet Jesus never asked me to earn His love by becoming somebody else. He met me where I was, and He began teaching me how to surrender the places within me that still needed His work. He challenged my reactions, my thoughts, my words, my fears, and my desire to defend myself. He showed me that being authentic does not mean refusing to change. It means allowing Him to change what needs to be changed without losing sight of the person He created me to be.

SCRIPTURE

"For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary the one to the other: so that ye cannot do the things that ye would."

Galatians 5:17

This is where the battle within becomes real. Following Jesus does not mean that every struggle disappears immediately. It means that we no longer have to face those struggles without Him. There are still moments when I become frustrated, overwhelmed, misunderstood, defensive, discouraged, or tempted to return to old patterns of thinking. The difference is that I now recognize those moments as opportunities to surrender rather than reasons to give up.

That does not mean every struggle is caused by sin. This is especially important when we talk about autism. Being autistic is not a sin that Jesus needs to remove. Different ways of processing the world are not moral failures. Sensory overload is not rebellion. Needing clarity is not a lack of faith. Becoming overwhelmed does not mean that a person has failed God. There are things about the way I experience the world that are simply part of my life, and I have learned that Jesus can meet me there as well.

What Jesus changes is deeper. He changes what I do with my frustration. He changes how I respond when I am misunderstood. He changes how I handle rejection. He changes whether I allow pain to become bitterness. He changes whether I live for the approval of people or for the approval of God. He changes whether my differences become an excuse to push people away or an opportunity to show them the grace, patience, truth, and love of Jesus Christ.

The battle is not against being different.

The battle is against becoming someone Jesus never called me to be.

That is one of the reasons the song Different by Micah Tyler became meaningful to me. Its message challenged me to look beyond the surface and ask whether I was actually allowing God to change me. The song speaks about surrender, transformation, and becoming so different through God that His work within us becomes visible to the people around us. Those ideas helped shape the question that became central to this book.

Am I simply different, or am I truly being changed?

That question forced me to look deeper than my personality, my autism, my circumstances, my history, or the way other people see me. None of those things can become a substitute for surrendering my heart to Jesus Christ. I can be different and still refuse to change. I can be misunderstood and still respond wrongly. I can have reasons for the way I react and still need Jesus to teach me how to respond differently.

The cross reminds me that Jesus is not finished with me. He sees everything that I am, everything I have experienced, everything I struggle to understand, and everything I still need to surrender. Yet He does not turn away from me. He calls me closer.

SCRIPTURE

"For that which I do I allow not: for what I would, that do I not; but what I hate, that do I."

Romans 7:15

There have been moments when I wished spiritual growth happened overnight. I wanted every weakness to disappear, every wound to heal, every misunderstanding to be corrected, and every difficult situation to make sense. Instead, God has repeatedly taught me that transformation is a journey of surrender. He does not simply change what happens around me. He works within me while I am walking through what happens around me.

Sometimes that means learning to pause before responding. Sometimes it means admitting that I was wrong. Sometimes it means forgiving someone even when they never apologize. Sometimes it means accepting that another person may never understand me the way I wish they would. Sometimes it means stepping away from the need to prove myself and trusting God to speak for me.

Those lessons are not always easy for me. They are especially difficult when I feel misunderstood because I know what it is like to have people interpret my actions without understanding what is happening beneath the surface. Autism has taught me that people can see the same behavior and completely misunderstand the reason behind it. Jesus has taught me that I must be careful not to make the same mistake with other people.

I may not always understand what someone else is carrying, just as they may not understand what I am carrying. That realization has changed the way I view grace. Jesus calls me to extend the same mercy to others that I continually need from Him.

REMEMBER

Jesus Is Changing More Than What People Can See

The evidence of transformation is not that I suddenly become easy for everyone to understand. The evidence is that Jesus is changing my heart, my responses, my priorities, and my desires. He is teaching me how to love when I feel rejected, forgive when I feel wounded, remain faithful when I feel alone, and surrender when I would rather remain in control.

The world may notice that something about us is different, but the greatest difference should point beyond us. When people see patience where there once was anger, forgiveness where there once was bitterness, humility where there once was pride, compassion where there once was judgment, and faith where there once was fear, they should be able to recognize that Jesus is doing something within us.

That is the kind of difference I want my life to show. I do not want people to look at me and simply see an autistic man who experiences the world differently. I want them to see a man who belongs completely to Jesus Christ. I want my life to point beyond my personality, my experiences, my strengths, my weaknesses, and even my differences so that people can see the One who is changing me.

Let Them See Jesus

I know that I am not perfect, and I know there are still areas of my life that Jesus is working on. There are still moments when I respond before I think, become frustrated when I feel misunderstood, or allow my emotions to become louder than my faith. But those moments do not have to become the final definition of who I am.

Jesus is still working.

The cross still reminds me that I am loved, forgiven, and called to something greater than remaining trapped in the person I used to be. His grace does not give me permission to remain unchanged. His grace gives me the strength to surrender and allow Him to continue the work He began.

So when people see me, I do not want them to see only my differences. I want them to see what Jesus has done with them. I want them to see that being different does not mean being without purpose, and being misunderstood does not mean being without value. I want them to see that a life surrendered to Jesus can become a testimony of His grace.

I cannot change myself into the person Jesus wants me to become. I can only surrender myself to Him and allow Him to do the changing.

That is the battle within.

Not the battle to become someone else, but the battle to surrender every part of myself to Jesus Christ so that He can finish what He started.

SCRIPTURE

"Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ."

Philippians 1:6

I Want To Be Changed

I do not want to spend my life trapped in the same patterns, controlled by the same fears, or defined by the same wounds. I do not want to gain the approval of the world while losing the things that matter most to God. I want Jesus Christ to have every part of me.

I want Him to change my heart before He changes my circumstances. I want Him to change my responses before He changes the people around me. I want Him to change my desires before He gives me what I think I want. I want Him to remove everything within me that keeps me from becoming more like Him while teaching me to embrace the person He created me to be.

I want to be different because Jesus has changed me.

And when the world sees that difference, I pray they do not stop with me.

I pray they see Him.
