INTRODUCTION

Does My Life Truly Reflect Jesus Christ?

Before we begin, there is a question worth answering honestly. Not with what we say, but with the life we actually live.

"Does my life truly reflect Jesus Christ?"

There are questions that can be answered quickly, and there are questions that require us to stop and examine ourselves. This is one of the latter. It asks us to look beyond what we say we believe and consider whether our faith is actually shaping the way we live.

It is possible to know the language of Christianity without allowing Jesus to change our lives. We can attend church, read Scripture, pray, sing, serve, and speak about our faith while still holding tightly to the parts of ourselves we do not want to surrender.

I know because I have had to confront that reality in my own life.

There have been times when I said I trusted Jesus while still trying to control the outcome. There have been times when I said I loved Him while struggling to obey Him. There have been moments when I wanted His grace without wanting His correction.

Those moments have taught me something important. The problem is not that Jesus has failed to change me. The problem is that I can resist the very work I am asking Him to do.

Change Begins With Surrender

Real transformation does not begin when we finally become strong enough to fix ourselves. It begins when we recognize that we cannot do it alone and become willing to place our lives completely in the hands of Jesus.

Surrender is not weakness. It is trust.

It means allowing Jesus to speak into our attitudes, decisions, relationships, habits, priorities, and desires. It means being willing to hear the truth even when the truth exposes something we would rather leave alone.

That kind of surrender can be uncomfortable because Jesus does not only comfort us. He also corrects us. He does not simply reassure us that we are loved. He calls us to follow Him.

Grace does not mean that nothing needs to change. Grace gives us the opportunity and strength to change.

THE QUESTION

Has Jesus Truly Changed Me?

This question is not intended to condemn anyone. It is an invitation to honesty. What happens when we stop measuring our faith by what we say and begin looking at the evidence of our lives?

Do we forgive differently? Do we respond differently when we are hurt? Do we handle correction differently? Do we love people differently? Do we trust Jesus when we cannot control the outcome?

The evidence of transformation is not perfection. It is direction.

A changed life will still have struggles. Following Jesus does not mean that temptation disappears, disappointment disappears, or failure disappears. It means that we no longer have to face those things as people who are trying to live entirely on our own strength.

When Jesus changes the heart, that change eventually begins to affect the life. Our words begin to change because our hearts are changing. Our responses begin to change because our priorities are changing. Our relationships begin to change because we are learning to love differently.

Sometimes the change is obvious. Sometimes it is so gradual that we barely recognize it until we look back and realize that Jesus has brought us farther than we thought.

That is why this book is not about perfection.

It is about direction.

It is about what happens when we stop asking Jesus to simply improve our circumstances and start asking Him to transform us.

Before You Continue

As you begin the chapters ahead, I want you to resist the temptation to read them only as information. Read them as an opportunity to examine your own heart.

When something challenges you, do not immediately dismiss it. Take it to Jesus. When something exposes an area of your life that needs attention, bring it before Him. When you recognize growth, give Him the glory.

You do not need to pretend that you have everything together. Jesus already knows where you are.

You simply need to be willing to come honestly.

That is where transformation begins.

A PERSONAL INVITATION

Look At Your Life Honestly

Before beginning Chapter One, consider your life as it is right now. What have you surrendered to Jesus, and what are you still trying to control? Where has He changed you, and where are you still resisting Him?

Do not answer for the person you want to become. Answer for the person you are today.

Then bring that answer to Jesus.

The purpose of this book is not to convince you that you are a failure. It is to remind you that Jesus is still working. The places where you need growth are not reasons to hide from Him. They are invitations to draw closer.

I am asking myself the same questions.

I am still learning to trust. I am still learning to surrender. I am still learning to obey when obedience is difficult. I am still learning that following Jesus means giving Him authority over every part of my life.

So I do not invite you into this journey as someone who has finished it. I invite you as someone who is still walking it.

One Question.

As you move into Chapter One, carry this question with you:

Has Jesus truly changed me?

Let the question remain open.

Let it challenge you.

Let it lead you back to Scripture.

And most importantly, let it lead you to Jesus.

The journey begins with honesty, but it continues through surrender.
