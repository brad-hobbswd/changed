INTRODUCTION

Does My Life Truly Reflect Jesus Christ?

Every transformed life begins with an honest question. Before we explore what it means to be changed, we must first be willing to examine our hearts and ask whether our lives truly reflect Jesus Christ.

"Does my life truly reflect Jesus Christ?"

Some questions deserve more than a quick answer because they require us to slow down, become honest with ourselves, and allow God to show us what we might otherwise avoid. There are questions we can answer with our minds, but there are other questions that must be answered with our lives.

This is one of those questions.

Does my life truly reflect Jesus Christ?

That question is not asking whether I know the right words, attend church regularly, understand Scripture, or know how to talk about Jesus. It reaches much deeper than outward appearances. It asks what is happening inside my heart and whether the presence of Jesus is actually changing the way I live.

If someone watched my life when nobody was applauding, when nobody was watching, and when I was faced with frustration, disappointment, temptation, misunderstanding, or conflict, would they see evidence that Jesus is changing me?

That question has challenged me personally because there have been times when my words sounded stronger than my actions. I have said that I trust Jesus and then struggled to surrender control. I have said that I love Him and then struggled to obey Him. I have spoken about grace while still holding onto things He was asking me to release.

That does not make the grace of Jesus less real. It reminds me why I need His grace in the first place.

The greatest miracle is not a changed circumstance.

It is a changed heart.

The world often tells us that change comes through determination, self improvement, success, or becoming a better version of ourselves. There is nothing wrong with growth, discipline, or wanting to improve, but the transformation Jesus offers reaches somewhere human effort cannot reach on its own.

Jesus does not simply want to make us look better on the outside. He wants to transform us from within. He changes the way we think, the way we respond, the way we love, the way we forgive, the way we handle failure, and the way we understand who we are. He changes our desires because He is changing the heart from which those desires come.

This is where the word changed becomes so important.

Being different is not necessarily the same thing as being changed.

I know what it means to be different. As an autistic person, I have experienced life in ways that other people have not always understood. I may process information differently, communicate differently, respond differently, or notice things that others might not notice. There have been moments when I have felt misunderstood simply because the way I experienced something did not match the way someone else expected me to experience it.

For a long time, experiences like those could make me question whether being different was something I needed to fix. I sometimes wondered whether I needed to become more like everyone else in order to be accepted, understood, or valued.

But Jesus began teaching me something different.

He did not call me

to become someone else.

He called me to become more like Him.

My autism is part of my story, but it is not the entirety of my identity. I am not defined by the expectations of other people, and I do not have to pretend that I experience the world exactly as they do. Jesus already knows me completely. He knows how I think, how I process, how I communicate, where I struggle, what overwhelms me, what brings me joy, and what I still need to learn.

Being autistic does not mean that I am exempt from growth, obedience, or accountability. Just as every believer must do, I have to allow Jesus to confront the places in my life that do not reflect Him. There are attitudes that need to change, responses that need correction, fears that need surrender, and desires that need to be placed beneath His will.

At the same time, not everything that makes me different needs to disappear. Some things need understanding rather than removal. Some things need acceptance rather than shame. Some things simply need to be surrendered to God so that He can use my life exactly as He created it.

That is one of the reasons this book is deeply personal to me.

THE HEART OF THIS JOURNEY

Different Is Not the Goal. Changed Is.

This book is not about becoming different simply for the sake of standing apart from everyone else. It is not about autism, personality, individuality, or trying to prove that we are unique.

It is about surrendering our lives to Jesus Christ and allowing Him to change us from the inside out while learning to faithfully live as the people He created us to be.

There have been many times when I wanted Jesus to change my circumstances without changing me. I wanted Him to remove the struggle without allowing the struggle to teach me. I wanted Him to change what was happening around me while I resisted what He was trying to change within me.

I think many of us can relate to that.

We ask God to change our situation, our relationships, our finances, our jobs, our circumstances, or the people around us. Yet sometimes the first person Jesus wants to change is us.

That does not mean that every difficult circumstance is our fault. It does not mean that every painful experience happened because we failed God. It means that even in circumstances we did not choose, Jesus can still do a work within us.

He can take pain and teach us compassion. He can take failure and teach us humility. He can take rejection and teach us where our identity truly belongs. He can take seasons of loneliness and teach us that His presence is enough. He can take the things we never expected and use them to deepen our trust in Him.

That is what transformation looks like.

It is not always dramatic. Sometimes it is quiet. Sometimes it happens one decision at a time. Sometimes nobody else notices the change because Jesus is working on something deep within the heart before it ever becomes visible to anyone else.

But eventually, what happens inside begins to appear outside.

The way we speak begins to change. The way we respond begins to change. The way we treat people begins to change. The things we once could not surrender begin to loosen their grip. The need for approval begins to fade because we discover that our identity is secure in Christ.

We begin to understand that following Jesus is not about becoming perfect. It is about becoming surrendered.

I am still learning that lesson.

I am still learning to listen when Jesus speaks. I am still learning to trust Him when I cannot see what He is doing. I am still learning to obey when obedience is uncomfortable. I am still learning to surrender the things I want to control. I am still learning that saying I love Jesus means very little if my life continually refuses to follow Him.

That is not a statement of hopelessness. It is a statement of dependence.

I need Jesus.

I need His grace when I fail. I need His correction when I am wrong. I need His strength when I am weak. I need His patience when I struggle to understand. I need His forgiveness when I fall short. Most of all, I need Him because I cannot transform my heart by myself.

Before you turn the next page,

I want you to stop and ask yourself one question.

Has Jesus truly changed me?

Do not answer too quickly.

Think about your life when nobody is watching. Think about the way you respond when you are frustrated. Think about how you treat people who cannot give you anything in return. Think about how you handle forgiveness, disappointment, temptation, correction, and uncertainty. Think about whether you are becoming more dependent upon Jesus or more dependent upon yourself.

This question is not meant to condemn you. It is an invitation to honesty.

If you discover areas of your life that do not reflect Jesus, do not run from Him. Run toward Him. Bring those areas into His presence. Ask Him to change what needs to be changed. Ask Him to teach you what needs to be learned. Ask Him to help you surrender what you have been holding onto.

And if you discover that Jesus really has been changing you, do not become proud of the change. Give Him the glory.

Because the goal of a changed life is not that people look at us and say, "Look how great you are."

The goal is that they look at our lives and see Jesus.

THE PRAYER OF THIS BOOK

When They See Me, Let Them See You.

Jesus, change what needs to be changed in me. Teach me to surrender what I keep trying to control. Teach me to listen when You speak, obey when You call, and trust You when I cannot understand.

Let my life become evidence of Your work in me. Let my words reflect You. Let my actions reflect You. Let my love reflect You. Let my forgiveness reflect You. Let my obedience reflect You.

When people see me, let them see You.

That is the heart of Changed.

Not becoming different simply because we want to stand out.

Not becoming someone else because the world tells us we should.

Not pretending that we have everything figured out.

Instead, it is about giving ourselves completely to Jesus Christ and allowing Him to continue the work He has already begun.

The eight chapters ahead will take us deeper into that journey. We will examine the heart of change, the cost of surrender, the battles that happen within us, the evidence that transformation produces, what it means to stand when standing is difficult, and what it looks like to continue living changed when the emotion of the moment has passed.

This journey will not ask you to pretend that you are perfect.

It will ask you to be honest.

It will ask you to surrender.

And above everything else, it will ask you to keep your eyes on Jesus.

Welcome to Changed.

The question has been asked.

Now the journey begins.
