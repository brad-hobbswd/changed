CHAPTER TWO

The Heart of the Change

Jesus begins transformation where no one else can see. Before our lives change on the outside, He begins His work within the heart.

Chapter Two • Where Transformation Begins
"Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right and steadfast spirit within me."

If I am serious about being changed by Jesus, I have to be willing to let Him work beneath the surface. It is possible to change the way I behave for a season without allowing Jesus to change the reasons behind my behavior. I can learn what to say, how to act, and what people expect from me while still carrying the same attitudes, fears, desires, and wounds within my heart.

Jesus wants something deeper than an outward appearance of change. He is not simply interested in making my behavior look different. He wants to transform the heart from which my behavior, decisions, words, and relationships flow.

The heart is where desires, motives, attitudes, and decisions come together. What I allow to remain within me will eventually influence the way I speak, respond, forgive, serve, and treat other people. That is why genuine transformation cannot remain an outward exercise. If the heart remains unchanged, outward change will eventually become difficult to maintain.

Jesus Begins Within.

I have learned that it is much easier to ask Jesus to change a situation than to ask Him to change me. When something hurts, I naturally want the pain removed. When someone treats me unfairly, I want the other person corrected. When circumstances become difficult, I want God to make them easier. Those desires are understandable, but they can also keep me from recognizing the work Jesus wants to do within me.

There is nothing wrong with bringing my circumstances to Jesus. He cares about what I experience, and He invites me to bring my burdens to Him. The danger comes when I become so focused on what needs to change around me that I refuse to consider what He may be revealing within me.

Sometimes the situation cannot be changed immediately, but my response can begin to change. Jesus can teach me patience while I wait, humility when I am corrected, forgiveness when I am hurt, and trust when I do not understand what He is doing.

THE HEART OF THE MATTER

What Is Jesus Changing In Me?

The question is not only what I want Jesus to change around me. I also have to ask what He is revealing within me and whether I am willing to let Him transform it.

That question requires honesty because the heart can hide things that our outward lives do not immediately reveal. I may believe that I have forgiven someone while continuing to replay the offense in my mind. I may say that I trust Jesus while still trying to control every possible outcome. I may speak about humility while becoming defensive whenever someone corrects me.

Jesus does not reveal these things to shame me. He reveals them because He wants to heal what has been wounded, correct what has become distorted, and transform what I have been unwilling to surrender.

That is where surrender becomes essential. Transformation requires more than recognizing what needs to change. It requires the willingness to place those things into the hands of Jesus and trust Him with what happens next.

Surrender Is More Than Giving Up.

Surrender means trusting Jesus with the parts of my life I would rather manage myself. It means allowing His will to have greater authority than my feelings, preferences, fears, and plans. It does not mean that I stop thinking, making decisions, or taking responsibility for my life. Instead, it means that I stop treating my own desires as the highest authority.

There comes a point when I have to be able to say, "Jesus, if this does not reflect You, change it in me." That prayer requires humility because sometimes I already know what He is asking me to surrender.

I may know that I need to forgive, yet still want to hold onto the hurt because it makes me feel justified. I may know that I need to apologize, yet pride may convince me that the other person should make the first move. I may recognize a habit that needs to change, yet comfort may make releasing it difficult. I may know that obedience is required while still hoping Jesus will give me another option.

Surrender becomes real when obedience becomes more important than getting my own way.

A HEART THAT IS WILLING

"Jesus, Search Me."

A surrendered heart does not pretend to have everything right. It remains willing to be examined, corrected, forgiven, and changed because it trusts Jesus enough to tell the truth about what is within.

This kind of honesty has changed the way I pray. Instead of only asking Jesus to fix what I can see, I want Him to reveal what I cannot see. I want Him to show me where pride has entered, where fear has taken control, where resentment has remained, and where my own desires have become more important than His will.

That does not mean that every struggle is evidence of a bad heart. We all experience emotions, difficulties, misunderstandings, disappointments, and circumstances that are outside our control. The question is what we do with those experiences and whether we allow them to determine the condition of our hearts.

When I am hurt, will I allow that hurt to become bitterness, or will I bring it to Jesus? When I am disappointed, will I allow disappointment to destroy my trust, or will I continue trusting Him? When I am corrected, will I immediately become defensive, or will I consider whether Jesus is using the moment to teach me something?

Those choices may appear small, but they reveal what is happening within the heart. What happens inside eventually finds a way to become visible outside.

Transformation Becomes Visible.

What Jesus changes within us eventually affects the way we live. A heart that is learning forgiveness will begin to produce forgiving actions. A heart that is learning humility becomes more willing to listen. A heart that is learning trust becomes less dependent upon controlling every possible outcome.

This kind of transformation does not happen overnight. Growth takes time, and some lessons have to be learned more than once. There may be days when I respond well and other days when I fall back into an old pattern. That does not mean Jesus has stopped working. It means I have another opportunity to return to Him and allow Him to continue the work.

Real transformation is not proven by never struggling again. It is revealed by what I do when I struggle. Do I justify myself, or am I willing to become honest? Do I hide from Jesus, or do I repent and return to Him? Do I give up when I fail, or do I allow Him to teach me and continue shaping my life?

Grace gives me room to grow without giving me permission to remain the same. Jesus does not condemn me because I am still growing, but neither does He ask me to become comfortable with the things He is calling me to surrender.

A Changed Heart Changes Direction.

A changed heart does not mean that every decision suddenly becomes easy. It means my direction begins to change. I become more concerned with following Jesus than protecting my pride. I become more willing to forgive than remain angry. I become more willing to obey than justify myself, and I begin to ask whether my choices reflect the One I say I follow.

The change may begin quietly, but it eventually affects everything. It affects my relationships because I begin to see people differently. It affects my words because I become more careful about what I say and why I say it. It affects my choices because I begin asking whether something honors Jesus instead of simply asking whether it is what I want.

Most importantly, it changes my relationship with God because surrender becomes more than an idea. It becomes a way of living.

THE QUESTION

What Am I Still Holding Onto?

Sometimes the next step in transformation is simply identifying the thing I have not yet surrendered and being honest enough to place it before Jesus.

Perhaps it is control. Perhaps it is resentment, fear, the need to be understood, an old habit, or a desire that has become more important than obedience. Whatever it is, Jesus already knows. I do not have to hide it from Him or pretend that it is not there.

I can bring it into His presence and ask Him for the strength to surrender it. I can trust that He knows what I need better than I do and that His correction is not intended to destroy me. It is intended to lead me into freedom.

Jesus Wants the Whole Heart.

It is easy to give Jesus the parts of my life that I am comfortable giving Him. The harder question is whether I will give Him the areas I still want to keep for myself.

Jesus is not asking for a portion of my heart. He calls me to love Him with everything I am. That kind of devotion requires trust because I cannot fully surrender while continuing to insist that I remain in control.

I am still learning this. There are things I have surrendered only to discover later that I was trying to take them back. There have been situations where I trusted Jesus one day and worried about the same situation again the next day. Instead of treating that as proof that surrender has failed, I am learning to see it as another opportunity to place my trust in Him.

Surrender is not a single decision that I make once and never revisit. It is a continuing response to the work Jesus is doing in me. Each day gives me another opportunity to say, "Jesus, this belongs to You."

THE HEART OF TRANSFORMATION

"Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right and steadfast spirit within me."

Psalm 51:10

That is the prayer I want to carry forward. I want Jesus to search my heart and show me what needs to change. I want the courage to face what He reveals and the humility to surrender it. I want Him to teach me to recognize the difference between what I need to accept about myself and what I need to allow Him to transform.

I want to become more patient, more forgiving, more truthful, more obedient, and more dependent upon Jesus. When I fail, I want to return to Him instead of hiding from Him. When I succeed, I want to remain humble. When I do not understand, I want to trust Him rather than allowing uncertainty to become an excuse for disobedience.

I do not want outward change without inward transformation. I do not want to learn how to look changed while protecting the parts of my heart that Jesus is asking me to surrender. I want Jesus to have my heart because that is where genuine change begins.

That Is Where the Change Begins.

The heart is where Jesus meets us honestly. It is where He reveals what needs to be surrendered, where He exposes what we have tried to hide, and where He begins forming something new within us. We may not see the entire process at once, and we may not understand every lesson along the way, but when we remain surrendered, Jesus continues His work.

I do not need to have everything figured out before I come to Him. I do not need to pretend that I am stronger than I am or hide the places where I still struggle. I simply need to remain willing to let Him search me, correct me, forgive me, and change me.

The question is no longer simply whether I want to change. The deeper question is whether I am willing to give Jesus access to the heart where change begins, trusting Him enough to surrender what He asks and follow Him wherever that surrender leads.
