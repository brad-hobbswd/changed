CHAPTER TWO

The Heart of the Change

Jesus does not begin His work by changing everything around us. He begins by changing what is happening within us.

Chapter Two • Where Transformation Begins
"Real change begins when I stop asking Jesus to change everything around me and allow Him to change me."

If Chapter One begins with the question, "Has Jesus truly changed me?" then this chapter has to ask where that change actually begins. It is easy to look at our circumstances and believe that if God would simply change what is happening around us, we would finally become the people we are supposed to be. We may think that if the right relationship comes along, the difficult person leaves, the financial situation improves, the opportunity finally appears, or the pain becomes easier to carry, then everything inside us will somehow become easier as well. Yet Jesus often begins His work in a place we cannot always see from the outside.

He begins with the heart.

The heart is more than an emotion. It is the center of who we are, including what we desire, what we believe, what we value, and what ultimately directs the way we live. That is why genuine transformation cannot be measured only by outward behavior. Someone can learn how to say the right things while still carrying bitterness inside. Someone can appear calm while secretly being controlled by anger. Someone can attend church faithfully while remaining unwilling to surrender his life completely to Jesus.

I have had to learn that lesson personally. There are things about myself that I can recognize more easily than others because I live inside my own thoughts every day. As an autistic person, I know that I can process situations differently, become deeply focused on certain things, communicate in ways that others may misunderstand, and experience certain environments or interactions differently from people around me. Those realities are part of my life, and understanding them has helped me understand myself better.

But understanding myself is not the same as surrendering myself.

That distinction has become increasingly important in my relationship with Jesus. Knowing why I respond a certain way can help me understand my behavior, but it does not automatically make every response right. Knowing that something is difficult for me does not mean Jesus cannot teach me how to respond faithfully within that difficulty. Understanding my own limitations does not remove my responsibility to love, forgive, repent, obey, and grow.

Jesus meets me where I am, but He loves me too much to leave me there.

The heart is where surrender begins.

Surrender is not always comfortable because it requires honesty. It requires me to stop defending everything about myself simply because it is familiar. It requires me to allow Jesus to show me the places where my motives are wrong, where pride has taken control, where fear is directing my decisions, where anger is shaping my responses, or where my desire to be understood has become more important than my willingness to understand others.

There are times when I want Jesus to affirm everything I feel. I want Him to tell me that every reaction I have is justified because I have been hurt, misunderstood, or treated unfairly. Sometimes I want Him to change the people around me so that I will not have to deal with the things they bring out of me.

But Jesus does something deeper.

He asks me to look at my own heart.

THE HEART OF THE MATTER

Jesus Changes From Within

The transformation Jesus brings is not simply an improvement in behavior. He changes the desires, attitudes, motives, and priorities that shape the way we live.

That kind of change can be difficult because the heart does not always reveal itself in the ways we expect. Sometimes we discover that the problem we thought belonged entirely to someone else also exposed something within us. Someone's words may have hurt me, but how I respond to those words still reveals something about my heart. Someone may have treated me unfairly, but what I choose to do with that pain still matters.

Jesus does not ask me to pretend that hurt does not hurt. He does not ask me to call wrong things right or to ignore injustice. He asks me to bring those things to Him rather than allowing them to become the things that control me.

That is where forgiveness becomes part of transformation. Forgiveness does not mean pretending nothing happened. It means refusing to allow what happened to become the master of my heart. It means trusting Jesus enough to release the right to carry revenge, bitterness, or hatred as though those things will somehow make me whole.

I have had to learn that being misunderstood does not give me permission to become bitter. Being rejected does not give me permission to reject everyone else. Being hurt does not give me permission to hurt people in return. Being different does not give me permission to stop growing.

Jesus can take every one of those experiences and use them to form something better within me.

He is not only changing

what I do.

He is changing why I do it.

That is where the difference between behavior and transformation becomes clear. I can force myself to behave a certain way for a while, especially when other people are watching. I can say the right words because I know what people expect to hear. I can appear patient even when I am angry. I can appear forgiving while secretly keeping a record of everything someone has done against me.

But eventually the heart reveals what is really there.

Jesus wants something deeper than performance. He wants truth within me. He wants my obedience to come from love rather than fear. He wants my forgiveness to come from understanding the forgiveness I have received. He wants my service to come from humility rather than the desire to be noticed. He wants my faith to remain when there is no audience to impress.

That is why private surrender matters so much.

Who am I when nobody is watching?

What do I think about when I am alone?

What do I hold onto when I am hurt?

What do I do when Jesus asks me to surrender something I do not want to surrender?

Those questions reveal more about the heart than any public appearance ever could.

A PERSONAL EXAMINATION

What Is Jesus Changing In Me?

Am I becoming more patient?

Am I becoming more forgiving?

Am I becoming more honest?

Am I becoming more willing to obey?

Am I becoming less controlled by the approval of people?

Am I becoming more willing to surrender my own way when Jesus shows me His?

These questions are not meant to condemn us. They are meant to help us become honest before God. The purpose of examining our hearts is not to discover that we have failed and then walk away in shame. The purpose is to recognize where Jesus is still working and invite Him to continue.

I know that I am far from perfect. I know there are still areas of my life where I need to grow. There are things I wish I handled differently, conversations I wish I could revisit, decisions I wish I had made differently, and moments when I allowed my emotions to speak louder than my faith. I could spend my life dwelling on those failures, or I can bring them to Jesus and allow Him to teach me through them.

That does not mean ignoring the consequences of our choices. Genuine repentance is more than feeling bad about what we have done. It means turning toward Jesus and allowing Him to change the direction of our lives.

Sometimes that change happens quickly. Sometimes it takes years. Sometimes Jesus changes something in us so gradually that we do not recognize how much He has done until we look back and realize that the person we once were would have responded very differently.

That kind of growth matters.

It reminds me that being changed is not always a dramatic moment. Sometimes it is the quiet decision to respond differently than I responded yesterday. Sometimes it is choosing forgiveness instead of bitterness. Sometimes it is choosing prayer instead of panic. Sometimes it is admitting that I was wrong instead of spending hours trying to prove that I was right. Sometimes it is simply getting back up after failure and saying, "Jesus, I still need You."

That is grace at work.

Jesus is not finished with me.

That sentence gives me hope because I know how easy it is to become discouraged when I see how far I still have to go. I can look at my weaknesses and believe they are proof that nothing has changed. I can remember my failures and allow them to become the definition of who I am.

But Jesus sees more than my failures.

He sees the person He is forming.

My autism does not prevent Him from working in me. My past does not prevent Him from working in me. My weaknesses do not prevent Him from working in me. The fact that I still struggle does not mean that transformation has failed.

It means I am still being formed.

The same Jesus who meets me in my strengths also meets me in my weaknesses. He understands the parts of my life that other people may not understand, and He is not intimidated by the places where I struggle. He does not ask me to hide those places from Him. He asks me to surrender them.

That is one of the most freeing things I have learned. I do not have to pretend before Jesus. I can tell Him when I am angry. I can tell Him when I am confused. I can tell Him when I am overwhelmed. I can tell Him when I do not understand why something happened. I can tell Him when I am afraid. I can tell Him when I have failed.

And then I can listen.

That may be one of the hardest parts of change.

Listening means that I cannot always decide beforehand what Jesus is going to say. If I truly want Him to change me, I have to be willing to hear Him when His answer challenges me. I have to be willing to let His Word correct me instead of using His Word only to confirm what I already believe.

I have spent enough time wanting God to change circumstances that I have learned to ask a different question.

"Jesus, what are You trying to change in me?"

That question changes everything because it moves my attention from circumstances to surrender. Instead of asking only why someone hurt me, I can ask what Jesus wants to teach me through the experience. Instead of asking why I am different, I can ask how He wants me to use the life He has given me. Instead of asking why people do not understand me, I can ask whether I am willing to understand them.

Those questions are not always easy, but they are transforming.

The goal is not for me to lose myself.

The goal is for me to surrender myself.

There is a difference.

Jesus does not erase personality, individuality, or the unique ways He has created people. He does not need every person to think, communicate, or experience life in exactly the same way. The body of Christ itself is made up of different people with different gifts, experiences, personalities, and callings.

What Jesus does call us to share is a heart surrendered to Him.

That is where real unity begins.

It is not found in all of us being the same. It is found in all of us belonging to the same Lord.

THE CHANGE JESUS WANTS

Not A Different Person.

A Surrendered Person.

Jesus is not asking me to become someone I was never created to be. He is asking me to surrender the person I am to Him so that my life can reflect His character.

That is why the title of this book matters so much to me. Changed does not mean that I have become unrecognizable. It means that Jesus is changing what needs to be changed while teaching me to live faithfully as the person He created me to be.

I can be autistic and still become more patient. I can process the world differently and still learn to love people well. I can struggle with communication and still become more intentional with my words. I can become overwhelmed and still learn to seek Jesus rather than allowing frustration to control me. I can be misunderstood and still refuse to become bitter.

That is transformation.

It is not about pretending that my struggles do not exist. It is about refusing to allow those struggles to have the final word over who I become.

Jesus gets the final word.

And when I surrender my heart to Him, He begins to change what I could never change by myself.

The heart comes first.

Before Jesus changes what people see, He changes what only He can see. Before transformation becomes visible in our actions, it begins in our motives. Before our lives become a testimony to other people, our hearts must become surrendered to God.

That is why I cannot simply ask Jesus to make me look different.

I have to ask Him to make me different within.

I have to let Him search the places I would rather keep hidden. I have to let Him challenge the attitudes I have learned to defend. I have to let Him correct the things I have convinced myself are acceptable. I have to allow Him to teach me that obedience is not about losing my freedom but about discovering what it means to trust the One who knows me better than I know myself.

And when I surrender, I begin to discover something beautiful.

Jesus is not trying to take my life away from me.

He is teaching me how to truly live it.

THE HEART OF TRANSFORMATION

"Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right and steadfast spirit within me."

Psalm 51:10

That is my prayer.

Not simply, "Jesus, change my circumstances."

Not simply, "Jesus, change the people who hurt me."

Not simply, "Jesus, make life easier."

But, "Jesus, change my heart."

Change the way I think. Change the way I respond. Change the way I love. Change the way I forgive. Change the way I trust. Change the way I obey. Change the things that keep me from becoming more like You.

And when I do not understand what You are doing, teach me to trust You anyway.

When I feel misunderstood, remind me that You understand me completely.

When I feel different, remind me that my identity is not found in the approval of people.

When I fail, teach me to repent rather than run away.

When I succeed, teach me humility.

When I am hurt, teach me forgiveness.

When I am afraid, teach me faith.

And when people look at my life, let them see more than my differences.

Let them see Jesus.

That is the heart of the change.

The journey is not finished because I still have much to learn. There are still places in my heart that need surrender, areas of my life that need growth, and moments when I will need to choose obedience again. But I no longer believe that change means becoming somebody else.

I believe change means becoming more like Jesus.

And that begins in the heart.

The next question is not whether change will cost us something. The next question is whether we are willing to surrender enough to discover what following Jesus will actually require.
