CHAPTER SEVEN

Living Changed

A life changed by Jesus is not revealed only in extraordinary moments. It is revealed in the ordinary moments of each day, in the way we respond to people, face our struggles, embrace who God created us to be, and choose to follow Him when no one else is watching.

"Being changed is not about becoming someone else. It is about becoming the person Jesus is calling you to be."

When many people think about living for Christ, they naturally think about the extraordinary moments. They imagine standing before large crowds, serving in a visible ministry, accomplishing something important, or being remembered for the work they have done. While God certainly calls some people into places where their faith can be seen by many, the evidence of a changed life is usually found much closer to home. It is found in the ordinary moments that most people never see, where our character is revealed and where our relationship with Jesus becomes more than something we talk about.

Living changed means allowing Jesus Christ to influence every part of our lives, including the conversations we have with our families, the decisions we make when nobody is watching, the way we respond when we are misunderstood, and the way we treat people who may never be able to give anything back to us. It means learning to forgive when holding on to resentment would feel easier, choosing honesty when compromise would be more convenient, and continuing to trust God even when we do not understand what He is doing.

These moments may seem ordinary, but they reveal whether the work Jesus has begun within us is continuing to shape the way we live.

SCRIPTURE

"Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God."

1 Corinthians 10:31

Jesus never separated faith from everyday life. He did not teach His followers to honor God only during worship services or when they were surrounded by other believers. He taught them to love their neighbors, care for those who were hurting, forgive those who wronged them, and remain faithful whether anyone else noticed or not. His life demonstrated that following God is not limited to what happens inside a church building. It reaches into every conversation, every relationship, every decision, and every difficult moment.

For much of my life, I have known what it feels like to experience the world differently from the people around me. As an autistic person, I have sometimes processed situations differently, communicated differently, noticed things that others did not notice, and struggled to understand why certain things seemed obvious to everyone else while they were difficult for me to understand. There have been moments when I felt misunderstood simply because the way I experienced something was not the way someone else experienced it.

For a long time, I wondered whether being different meant something was wrong with me. I questioned why I could not always respond the way people expected me to respond or why I sometimes felt as though I was trying to understand a world that seemed to come naturally to everyone else. There were moments when I wished I could simply blend in, avoid being misunderstood, and become more like the people around me.

But Jesus began teaching me something that I needed to understand. My autism does not determine my worth, and being different does not mean that I am broken. I do not need Jesus to erase the way He created me in order to prove that He is working in my life. What I need is for Jesus to transform my heart, guide my choices, shape my character, and teach me how to use the life He has given me for His glory.

Jesus Did Not Call Me To Become Someone Else.

He Called Me To Become More Like Him.

That distinction changed the way I understood my own story. The goal was never to become different simply for the sake of being different. The goal was never to prove that I could stand apart from everyone else. The goal was to surrender myself completely to Jesus and allow Him to change what needed to be changed within me while teaching me to embrace the person He created me to be.

That is also why the message behind Micah Tyler's song Different became meaningful to me as I began developing this book. The song challenged me to think beyond simply being different and to consider what it would look like for the life of Jesus to become visible through my own life. It reminded me that the most important difference is not the way I appear to other people. The greatest difference is what Jesus is doing within me.

My autism may be part of my story, but it is not the whole story. My personality may influence the way I communicate, think, process information, and experience the world, but it does not determine the condition of my heart. Jesus is still calling me to love, forgive, serve, obey, grow, and surrender. He is still teaching me how to respond when I am frustrated, how to remain patient when I feel misunderstood, and how to show grace when someone does not understand me.

DAILY WALK

Living Changed Does Not Mean Becoming Someone Else

A changed life does not require us to erase the unique ways God has created us. It requires us to surrender every part of ourselves to Jesus so that our thoughts, attitudes, choices, relationships, and responses increasingly reflect His character.

There are still moments when I become frustrated because I feel misunderstood. There are still situations where I have to stop and think carefully about how I should respond instead of simply reacting to what I am feeling. There are still days when I become overwhelmed, tired, or discouraged. Being changed by Jesus has not made me immune to those experiences. Instead, He is teaching me how to walk through them without allowing them to control who I become.

That is where living changed becomes real. It is easy to speak about faith when everything is peaceful, but our character is revealed when life becomes difficult. When someone misunderstands us, do we respond with grace? When we are treated unfairly, do we surrender our desire for revenge to God? When we are disappointed, do we allow bitterness to take root? When we fail, do we run away from Jesus in shame, or do we return to Him and allow His grace to restore us?

The answer to those questions is not always revealed in one dramatic moment. More often, it is revealed through a thousand ordinary decisions made over the course of a lifetime.

SCRIPTURE

"He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much."

Luke 16:10

That truth reminds me that every day matters. Every conversation matters. Every decision matters. Every opportunity to show kindness matters. Even the moments that seem insignificant can become opportunities to reflect Jesus Christ.

The longer I follow Jesus, the more I realize that transformation is not about becoming perfect. It is about becoming surrendered. I am still learning. I am still growing. I still make mistakes, and I still have moments when I respond in ways that do not reflect the person I want to become. However, I am learning not to use those failures as an excuse to remain the same. Instead, I bring them to Jesus and allow Him to continue His work in me.

That is grace.

Grace does not tell us that sin does not matter. Grace reminds us that failure does not have to be the end of our story. When we fall, Jesus calls us back. When we become discouraged, He reminds us of His faithfulness. When we recognize something in ourselves that needs to change, He gives us the courage to surrender it.

GRACE

Jesus Changes The Heart Without Erasing The Person

Jesus does not need to destroy our personality, erase our individuality, or make us identical to everyone around us in order to transform us. He changes the heart so that the person He created us to be can increasingly reflect His love, His truth, His compassion, and His character.

The more I understand God's grace in my own life, the more I realize that I cannot look at other people through the lens of superiority. I cannot assume that because someone communicates differently, thinks differently, worships differently, or experiences life differently, they are somehow less valuable. Every person carries a story that I may never fully understand, and every person deserves to be treated with the love and dignity that Jesus taught us to give.

That does not mean that truth no longer matters. Jesus never compromised truth, but He never used truth as an excuse to stop loving people. He confronted sin while extending compassion. He spoke with conviction while reaching toward those who were hurting. He demonstrated that truth and love were never enemies.

As Jesus continues changing me, I want that same balance to become visible in my life. I want to stand firmly for what I believe while still remembering that the person standing across from me is someone God loves. I want to speak truth without becoming cruel, defend what is right without becoming hateful, and remain faithful to Jesus without believing that I am better than anyone else.

There will be days when I succeed beautifully, and there will also be days when I fail. There will be moments when I respond with patience and moments when frustration gets the better of me. There will be times when I feel confident in what God is doing and other times when I struggle to understand His plan. None of those moments change the faithfulness of Jesus.

When I fail, I do not want to hide from Him. I want to run toward Him. I want to confess what I have done, receive His forgiveness, learn from the experience, and continue walking. The enemy may try to use failure to convince me that I have not changed, but Jesus uses those moments to remind me that I still need Him.

SCRIPTURE

"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness."

1 John 1:9

Every new day gives me another opportunity to surrender my heart to Jesus. It gives me another opportunity to love more deeply, forgive more freely, trust more completely, and become more like Him. The goal is not to wake up tomorrow and suddenly become a completely different person. The goal is to wake up tomorrow and surrender that day to Jesus.

When I look back over my own journey, I can see that many of the greatest changes God has made in me did not happen suddenly. They happened gradually through experiences I did not always understand at the time. Some lessons came through disappointment. Some came through rejection. Some came through being misunderstood. Some came through realizing that I could not control how other people viewed me, but I could control whether I allowed their opinions to determine how I lived.

My autism has taught me that the world does not always understand the way I experience things, but my relationship with Jesus has taught me that I do not have to be understood by everyone in order to be secure in who I am. I do not have to constantly explain myself to prove my worth. I do not have to become someone else to earn acceptance. My identity is ultimately found in Jesus Christ.

That does not mean I stop growing. It means I grow from a place of security rather than shame. I can recognize the areas where Jesus is still working in me without believing that my differences make me less valuable. I can acknowledge my weaknesses without allowing them to become my identity. I can celebrate the ways God has created me while remaining completely surrendered to His transforming work.

REFLECTION

Faithfulness Is Built One Day At A Time

God's work in our lives is often accomplished quietly. Day by day, choice by choice, He teaches us to trust Him, follow Him, and reflect Him more clearly. Never underestimate what Jesus can accomplish through a heart that continues to surrender to Him.

Living Changed Every Day

Perhaps you are waiting for God to do something extraordinary in your life, and you have begun to believe that nothing is happening because you cannot see a dramatic change. Do not overlook what He is doing today. He may be working through the ordinary moments that you have learned to dismiss as insignificant.

He is present in the prayers no one else hears, in the obedience nobody applauds, in the forgiveness nobody recognizes, and in the quiet decisions you make to follow Him when taking the easier path would be more comfortable. He is present when you choose patience instead of anger, grace instead of resentment, truth instead of compromise, and faith instead of fear.

The changed life is not built upon one dramatic experience. It is built through countless ordinary moments in which we choose, again and again, to follow Jesus Christ. Over time, those choices become the testimony of a life that has been transformed.

People may never remember every word I spoke or every accomplishment I achieved. They may not understand every part of my story, and they may never fully understand what it has been like for me to experience life as an autistic person. However, I pray that when people encounter me, they see something that goes beyond my differences. I pray they see the evidence of Jesus.

I want them to see patience where there could have been frustration, forgiveness where there could have been bitterness, compassion where there could have been judgment, and faith where circumstances could have produced fear. I want my life to communicate something that my words alone never could.

I Want Them To See Jesus.

That is what it means for me to live changed.

I do not need to become someone else to prove that Jesus has changed me. I do not need to hide the fact that I am autistic, and I do not need to be ashamed of the ways I experience the world differently. I simply need to surrender who I am to the One who created me and allow Him to continue transforming my heart.

Because being different is not the final destination.

Being changed by Jesus is.

When people see my life, I pray they do not simply see Bradley. I pray they see someone who has been forgiven, someone who is still growing, someone who still struggles, someone who still needs grace, and someone who has decided that Jesus is worth surrendering everything to.

That is the life I want to live.

A life that does not point back to itself, but points to Jesus.

A life that does not seek its own glory, but gives glory to the One who changed it.

A life that is not simply different, but genuinely changed.

SCRIPTURE

"Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ."

Philippians 1:6
